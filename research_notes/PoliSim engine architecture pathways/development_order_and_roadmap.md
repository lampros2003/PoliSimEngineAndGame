## 1. Game-development evidence on "engine first": postmortems, successes, stalls, and what distinguishes them

### Takeaway
The documented successes with custom technology (Factorio, Noita, Songs of Syx, Democracy, Paradox titles) all had a playable game driving the technology very early or had technology that *was* the gameplay hook, and they took 6 to 9 years; the clearest documented failure (Introversion's Subversion) is precisely the "tech for years, no core game" pattern. Democracy 4 itself shipped with a simulation that costs on the order of a microsecond per equation and was optimised with a profiler after the design existed, which is direct evidence that this genre does not need a high-performance engine to exist before the model does.

### Cited Findings

**The canonical stall: technology before core game**
- [DEV] Introversion shelved *Subversion* in October 2011 after roughly six years of work. Chris Delay said the team had spent too much time creating the tech and not enough time developing core gameplay, and described "a massive gaping hole" where the heart of the game should have been. His stated lesson: get to the core game as quickly as possible, whatever the cost. The studio then made *Prison Architect* (idea from a 2010 Alcatraz visit). — [MCV/Develop](https://www.mcvuk.com/subversion-dropped-after-six-years-development/); [PC Gamer interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/introversions-chris-delay-on-shifting-from-subversion-to-prison-architect-i-wanted-to-build-alcatraz); [Wikipedia: Subversion](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Subversion_(video_game))

**Successes that built custom technology**
- [DEV] Factorio: the first version was written in Java; kovarex wrote that it took him "a whole 12 days to realize that it is not a good idea, and I switched to C++". Friday Facts #184 (March 2017) marked exactly five years since the initial commit (so roughly March 2012) and released the pre-alpha 0.1 build. — [Friday Facts #184](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-184); see also [Alt-F4 #56, Factorio's Early Days](https://alt-f4.blog/ALTF4-56)
- [DOC] Factorio's optimisation posts span at least March 2017 (FFF #182) to July 2024 (FFF #421 "Optimizations 2.0", plus FFF #415 in June 2024), i.e. optimisation continued 5 to 12 years after the first commit. — [Factorio blog, optimisation search](https://factorio.com/blog/search/optimization); [FFF #421 discussion](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=615026)
- [COMMUNITY] A forum poster claims it took Wube five years of engine optimisation to take a megabase from 7 UPS to 70 UPS. Not verified against an official post. — [Factorio forums](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=575951)
- [DEV] Factorio's FFF #366 (June 2021) is titled "The only way to go fast, is to go well!"; in its thread kovarex describes TDD as rapidly alternating between extending tests and making them pass, a home-built test-dependency system, and a split between mocked unit tests and integration tests. — [FFF #366 thread](https://forums.factorio.com/98893)
- [DOC] Noita: custom C++ engine "Falling Everything" (Box2D, FMOD as third-party parts). Engine work started as early as 2011, game-design work in January 2013, Early Access on 24 September 2019, 1.0 on 15 October 2020. The technology preceded the design by about two years and the whole effort took about nine. — [Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-nolla-games-i-noita-i-); [DiGRA paper on Noita](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/1938/1937); [GDC 2019 talk](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025695/Exploring-the-Tech-and-Design)
- [DOC] Songs of Syx: a one-person studio (Gamatron; developer named as Jakob de Laval), written in Java on LWJGL, in development since 2014 or 2015 (sources differ), described as a prototype for six years before Early Access in September 2020. — [GamingOnLinux, 2019](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2019/07/songs-of-syx-a-city-builder-with-empire-management-tactical-battles-and-rpg-elements); [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1162750); [ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/games/songs-of-syx); [docs](https://songs-of-syx.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)

**Positech / Democracy (the project's direct inspiration)**
- [DEV] Democracy 4's simulation is built from neural-style connection objects (`SIM_NeuralEffect` in the code), each with a host, a target and an equation; the equation processor runs every turn over a very large number of them and largely sets the game's speed. In December 2021 Cliff Harris profiled it and stopped calling `atof()` on constant values each turn (caching them instead): the whole pre-calculation function went from 1.50 s to 0.85 s, which he describes as roughly a 33% speedup of all processing. — [Cliffski's blog, 29 Dec 2021](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/12/29/speeding-up-democracy-4-simulation-processing-atof-is-slow/)
- [DEV] November 2022 follow-up: new-game start-up processes about 640,000 equations (2,000 voters with about 10 connections each, pre-processed 32 times). He notes Democracy 4 is not exactly a slow game and that he is optimising "for fun". Asked about GPU compute for the neural-network-like sim, he declined because GPU processing adds "an additional exciting way for your game to crash" or hit incompatibilities, and said the game is already quick for many players. — [Cliffski's blog, 5 Nov 2022](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2022/11/05/optimization-for-fun/)
- [DEV] March 2023: the polls screen went from 59 fps to 228 fps mainly by cutting draw calls, i.e. rendering, not simulation, was the visible bottleneck. — [Cliffski's blog, 15 Mar 2023](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2023/03/15/speeding-up-my-game-from-59fps-to-228-fps/)
- [DOC] Democracy 3 was described at preview as structurally a neural network with player-overridable policies and equations; the reporter notes that validating the accuracy of such a model is "incredibly hard to research". — [PC Gamer hands-on](https://pcgamer.com/democracy-3-hands-on-losing-elections-with-land-mines-in-a-complex-politics-sim/2)

**Paradox (Victoria 3)**
- [DEV] Victoria 3 Dev Diary #76 (Performance): a tick is six hours (a quarter of a day); work is split into yearly, monthly, weekly, daily and regular tick tasks; early-2022 gains (before the October 2022 release window) came largely from "parallelization of the modifier node calculations". — [Dev Diary #76](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-76-performance)
- [DEV] Dev Diary #105: migration "works by regularly splitting Pops up", which "results in a higher number of Pops and worsened performance as the game goes on"; the 1.6 changes cut late-game pop counts by about 15 to 20% in internal tests. The late-game performance problem was a property of the model's entity growth and was fixed by changing the model. — [Dev Diary #105 thread](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-105-migration-in-1-6.1624226/page-6)

**"Make games, not engines" and its counter-arguments**
- [OPINION] The origin essay notes that "engine" has no strict definition and that a game can be built from custom or reusable components. — [Write Games, Not Engines](https://geometrian.com/projects/blog/write_games_not_engines.html)
- [OPINION] Counter-position: every game has an engine; making a game "without an engine" is just making a game, and the engine arises from the game's needs; a custom engine can be tailored to one game while a general engine must serve all kinds. — [Falsehoods about Engine-less Game Development](https://tlalice.flounder.online/thoughts/enginelessgamedev.gmi); [OSnews: Making video games in 2025 without an engine](https://www.osnews.com/story/142403/making-video-games-in-2025-without-an-engine/); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/dont-blame-game-engines-for-bad-games-developers-say/)
- [OPINION] Pro-advice position: engine skills and game skills overlap little; learning to make games by making an engine is like learning guitar by building guitars; custom engines are self-imposed technical debt needing continuous maintenance. — [Sean Middleditch](https://seanmiddleditch.github.io/makes-games-not-engines-to-learn-engines); [Game Developer: Why I'm not rolling my own engine](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/why-i-m-not-rolling-my-own-engine)

**Empirical studies of game postmortems**
- [DOC] Petrillo et al. (2009), 20 postmortems: unrealistic or over-ambitious scope 75% of projects; feature creep 75%; features cut during development 70%; design-phase problems 65%; delays or optimistic schedules 65%; technological problems 60%. — [Petrillo et al. 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/what-went-wrong-a-survey-of-problems-in-game-development-3m7iflo7ak)
- [DOC] Washburn et al. (2016), 155 Gamasutra postmortems: the four most frequent "went wrong" categories were obstacles (37 postmortems), schedule (25), development process and game design; "game design" was the most frequent "went right" (50), associated with having a hook and a clear vision; the authors recommend a well-defined concept before development rather than ad hoc design, and cite a prototype (Splinter Cell) that "helped determine resources and scheduling" and became the base for production. — [Washburn et al. 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/what-went-right-and-what-went-wrong-an-analysis-of-155-30e5mpqcdd)
- [DOC] Callele et al. (2005): many game-project failures trace to the transition from pre-production to production. — [Callele et al. 2005](https://scispace.com/papers/requirements-engineering-and-the-creative-process-in-the-1qi01gb4lu)

### Inferences
- **What distinguishes the successes [OWN].** (1) A playable game existed almost immediately and pulled the technology along: Factorio's language decision was made on day 12, when almost nothing was sunk, and the heavy optimisation came years later against real factories. (2) Where technology did come first (Noita), the technology was itself the hook (every pixel simulated) and could not be bought, and it still took about nine years. (3) The developers were reusing or evolving a codebase across titles (Positech, Paradox), so "engine" work was amortised over shipped games rather than done speculatively. (4) They tolerated very long timelines. Subversion is the same pattern without (1): six years, no core loop.
- **Strongest case for engine-first [OWN].** Some properties are expensive to retrofit (determinism, threading model, data layout, save format; see section 4), and the Victoria 3 record shows a shipped Paradox title still fighting simulation scaling. Factorio's and Noita's distinctive scale would not exist on a generic stack. A developer who knows these constraints from day one avoids a painful rewrite. And the 12-day Java episode shows a wrong foundation is noticed fast only if someone is looking.
- **Strongest case against [OWN].** None of the cited successes built a general high-performance engine *before* having a model or a game; Positech's own figures show the Democracy model is small and was optimised afterwards with a profiler; Paradox's worst performance problem was a model property (pop fragmentation) that no pre-built engine would have predicted; and 75% of postmortems report scope problems versus 60% technology problems, with Subversion as the worked example of the cost.
- For PoliSim the hook is believability and legibility of cause and effect, not raw entity count, so the "technology is the hook" justification applies only if the design genuinely commits to agent-scale populations or heavy in-game Monte Carlo [OWN].

- No first-hand statement was retrieved on how Cliff Harris originally prototyped or balanced Democracy 1 to 3 (search budget exhausted; positech.co.uk could not be opened). Team size and timeline for Democracy 3 and 4 are unverified here.
- Dwarf Fortress, Prison Architect's engine, Banished, RimWorld/Ludeon and the history of Paradox's Clausewitz/Jomini engines could not be researched in this session. One search for Tarn Adams statements on performance regrets found nothing citable.

## 2. Prototyping practice for simulation games: spreadsheets, Machinations, vertical slices, walking skeletons

### Takeaway
The game-design literature and the one controlled experiment found both favour getting an executable, cheap version of the *mechanics* in front of people before investing in infrastructure, but they also show the cost: prototypes produce less coherent designs and are harder to manage. The walking-skeleton idea (a thin, real, end-to-end slice that is kept, not thrown away) is the standard reconciliation.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Machinations (Dormans 2011): diagrams represent the flow of resources that makes up a game's internal economy, which "has a large impact on the emergent gameplay of most simulation games, strategy games and board games"; the paper shows the diagrams "can be used [to] simulate and balance games before they are built". — [Dormans 2011](https://scispace.com/papers/simulating-mechanics-to-study-emergence-in-games-v7dak13j0y); textbook treatment in [Adams & Dormans 2012](https://scispace.com/papers/game-mechanics-advanced-game-design-23pl62mlvp)
- [DOC] Klint & van Rozen (2013) formalise a subset as a DSL, arguing that designers and engineers "both lose time when adjustments best understood by designers are implemented by engineers". This is the argument for a data-driven, designer-editable model definition. — [Micro-Machinations](https://scispace.com/papers/micro-machinations-a-dsl-for-game-economies-1ly92khqb6)
- [DEV] Offworld Trading Company (Soren Johnson, Mohawk): the prototype was released to pre-order "Founders" under NDA specifically to give the team a feedback loop to keep iterating; Johnson's design notes say the claim system changed least from the earliest prototype to the finished game; Early Access began 12 February 2015, release was 28 April 2016, and Early Access lasted 14 months, about half of the project. His GDC talk covers the path "from conception to prototyping to Early Access to final release". — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/offworld-trading-company-prototype-is-now-available-as-a-founders-elite-edition-preorder-bonus); [Designer Notes](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-2-free-markets/); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/civilization-ivs-lead-designer-wants-you-to-financially-ruin-your-enemies/2); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024492/contactUs)
- [DOC] Boehm, Gray & Seewaldt (1984), seven teams building the same 2,000 to 4,000-line product: prototyping teams produced products with roughly equivalent performance but about 40% less code and 45% less effort; prototyped products rated somewhat lower on functionality and robustness but higher on ease of use and learning; specifying produced more coherent designs and software that was easier to integrate. — [Boehm et al. 1984](https://scispace.com/papers/prototyping-versus-specifying-a-multiproject-experiment-1iy2conlxc)
- [DOC] Alavi (1984): prototyping improved communication between users and designers, but designers who prototyped had difficulty managing and controlling the design process. — [Alavi 1984](https://scispace.com/papers/an-assessment-of-the-prototyping-approach-to-information-3rafhuease)
- [DOC] Davis (1992) "operational prototyping": combine throwaway and evolutionary prototyping "by layering a rapid prototype over a solid evolutionary base". — [Davis 1992](https://scispace.com/papers/operational-prototyping-a-new-development-approach-1ozqrbce36)
- [OPINION, secondary attribution] Walking skeleton (Alistair Cockburn): a tiny implementation of the system that performs a small end-to-end function; it need not use the final architecture but should link the main architectural components; it is production code that grows, not a throwaway prototype. — [Xebia](https://xebia.com/blog/the-technical-walking-skeleton/); [Semantic Anchors](https://llm-coding.github.io/Semantic-Anchors/anchor/walking-skeleton)
- [DOC] Murphy-Hill et al. (2014; 14 interviews, 364 survey responses): game developers are wary of automated tests because tests go obsolete as creative direction shifts, and tests are harder to write because the UI is hard to separate from the rest of the game and the state space is hard to explore; non-determinism (multithreading, AI, randomness) makes bugs hard to reproduce. — [Murphy-Hill et al. 2014](https://scispace.com/papers/cowboys-ankle-sprains-and-keepers-of-quality-how-is-video-7ew41sq3n9)
- [DOC] Tschang (2005; 65 project reports): game development shows "experiential" processes: frequent milestones and testing, multiple design iterations. Kanode & Haddad (2009): engaging gameplay is sought "through the use of prototypes and iterations". — [Tschang 2005](https://scispace.com/papers/videogames-as-interactive-experiential-products-and-their-23bnzuu39d); [Kanode & Haddad 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/software-engineering-challenges-in-game-development-2ed9ekiqdh)

### Inferences
- **Failure modes by approach [OWN].**

| Approach | Typical failure modes | Evidence it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Engine first | No playable build for years; engine generalises over data shapes that the model later does not have; motivation and funding run out before the model is tested | Subversion; Petrillo scope figures; Acton's "different data, different problem" (section 4) |
| (b) Model first in Python/Julia/spreadsheet | Prototype "works in a notebook" but is never tested as a game; translation to the engine introduces divergence; prototype quietly becomes production; two codebases to maintain | Donkin 2017 replication divergence (section 3); Boehm's robustness and coherence penalties; two-language literature |
| (c) Thin vertical slice in final language | Slow model iteration if the final language is heavy; early architecture locked in before the model's shape is known; throwaway UI becomes permanent | Alavi's control problems; Rust benchmark weaknesses for agents (section 8) |

- Murphy-Hill's finding that tests rot when "fun" requirements shift, and that UI entanglement blocks testing, argues for a headless, deterministic simulation core with a stable numeric contract: that part of a sim game *can* be tested like ordinary software even while the game design on top keeps moving [OWN].
- Davis's "rapid prototype over a solid evolutionary base" is, in effect, the hybrid of (b) and (c): a disposable exploration layer (notebook, spreadsheet, Machinations-style model) on top of a kept, tested core [OWN].
- A spreadsheet or Machinations model answers "does the economy behave?" but not "is it fun and legible to play?"; OTC's team treated external play of a prototype as the feedback loop and spent half the project in public iteration [OWN].

### Gaps
- Hunt & Thomas's "tracer bullets" (The Pragmatic Programmer) could not be sourced in this session; only secondary descriptions of Cockburn's walking skeleton were found, not his own text.
- No source was retrieved for Paradox's internal prototyping practice, Sid Meier's "find the fun", or a detailed account of the OTC prototype's technology and duration. The statement that Early Access was "about half of the project" is from a search summary attributed to Johnson's postmortem and should be re-checked.
- No evidence was found either way on spreadsheet prototypes specifically for political or macroeconomic games.

## 3. Scientific and policy-modelling practice: two-language problem, prototype-to-production paths, ODD/POM/TRACE, verification

### Takeaway
Modelling best practice is unambiguous about order: objectives and conceptual model first, implementation next, verification, sensitivity analysis and validation after, with runtime optimisation an optional late step; and independent re-implementation is treated as a verification tool, not a waste. Production paths in the wild are of two kinds: a slow prototype later re-implemented and validated against the original (the Austrian macro ABM to BeforeIT.jl), or a single high-level language with compiled kernels from day one (Covasim).

### Cited Findings

**Two-language problem**
- [DOC] Julia's designers explicitly set out to refute the "law" that "one must prototype in one language and then rewrite in another language for speed or deployment". — [Bezanson et al. 2017, SIAM Review](https://scispace.com/papers/julia-a-fresh-approach-to-numerical-computing-3dxw5qfrrd)
- [DOC] HPC practitioners describe the standard pattern as prototyping in Python or MATLAB, then porting to C/C++ or Fortran, and present Julia as bridging it. — [Kaus et al. 2023](https://scispace.com/papers/using-julia-for-the-next-generation-of-hpc-ready-software-22v90nw9); [de Montserrat et al. 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/building-an-ecosystem-of-computational-geosciences-software-1gvz3uubl6)
- [OPINION] Critiques: a Rust-community post argues Julia cannot match Rust's static analysis and that no language fully solves the problem; forum commenters note that translation both slows work and introduces bugs, and one argues AI coding tools make the two-language problem "no big deal, at least for now". — [Julia Discourse: Rust vs Julia](https://discourse.julialang.org/t/blog-post-rust-vs-julia-in-scientific-computing/101711/1); [Julia Discourse: definition thread](https://discourse.julialang.org/t/two-language-problem-what-is-it/82925); [Julia Discourse: Wired thread](https://discourse.julialang.org/t/wired-python-is-so-slow-can-julia-solve-the-two-language-problem/138165)
- [DOC] A study of developer questions about Julia found recurring difficulty with cross-language transfer, types and package resolution. — [Bose et al. 2022](https://scispace.com/papers/what-questions-do-developers-ask-about-julia-9q41x8ep)

**Prototype-to-production in economic and epidemiological ABMs**
- [DOC] BeforeIT.jl (Banca d'Italia, arXiv February 2025) re-implements the macro ABM of Poledna et al., whose original implementation was in MATLAB and served as the base for other variants. The authors report the Julia version is orders of magnitude faster than MATLAB and significantly faster than MATLAB-generated C, crediting JIT compilation and better sampling algorithms. (Authors' own benchmarks.) — [BeforeIT.jl paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13267)
- [DOC] The Italian-economy adaptation began by replicating the original Austrian Monte Carlo simulations as a preliminary step, then calibrated for Italy. — [Di Domenico et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/a-large-scale-data-driven-agent-based-model-for-the-italian-2jpoaqwrks)
- [DOC] Covasim (COVID-19 ABM used for policy in more than a dozen countries) was written in pure Python with NumPy and Numba; it was built "in a few weeks", "an order of magnitude faster than the typical model development process", and the authors claim performance comparable to C++; realistic scenarios run on a laptop in under a minute. They report a trade-off: vectorised state arrays instead of object-oriented agents improved performance but increased development time and complexity. — [Kerr et al. 2022, SciPy proceedings](https://scispace.com/papers/python-vs-the-pandemic-a-case-study-in-high-stakes-software-3pm11x0l); [Kerr et al. 2021, PLOS Comput Biol](https://scispace.com/papers/covasim-an-agent-based-model-of-covid-19-dynamics-and-92fdbabj0s)
- [DOC] Repast4Py: a Python ABM toolkit layered on HPC machinery using Numba, NumPy and the Python C-API, i.e. high-level model code over compiled kernels. — [Collier et al. 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/experiences-in-developing-a-distributed-agent-based-modeling-4d4hsh85zr)

**Best-practice guidance on order of work**
- [DOC] ODD (Grimm et al. 2020 second update): the protocol is used by some groups "as a workflow for model design", not only as documentation; the update adds guidance on rationale, evaluation and pointing from the description to code. — [Grimm et al. 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/the-odd-protocol-for-describing-agent-based-and-other-1zkv2up97k)
- [DOC] Pattern-oriented modelling: start by identifying patterns observed at multiple scales that characterise the system for the problem at hand; use them to decide which entities, variables and processes the model needs, then to test and select submodels, then to calibrate; the aim is "the right level of complexity". — [Grimm & Railsback 2012](https://scispace.com/papers/pattern-oriented-modelling-a-multi-scope-for-predictive-33wbsytap2); [Grimm et al. 2005, Science](https://scispace.com/papers/pattern-oriented-modeling-of-agent-based-complex-systems-43ebw1z7y9)
- [DOC] TRACE (Schmolke et al. 2010): documentation follows the modelling cycle in order: formulation of objectives, conceptual model, implementation and verification, sensitivity analysis, validation against independent data, uncertainty analysis; the review found elements of good practice "have long been identified but are widely ignored". — [Schmolke et al. 2010](https://scispace.com/papers/ecological-models-supporting-environmental-decision-making-a-2kyjmkcd1b)
- [DOC] TRACE modelling notebooks (Ayllón et al. 2021) list code design, implementation "and (possibly) runtime optimization" as things to log within the cycle; optimisation is optional and not the first step. — [Ayllón et al. 2021](https://scispace.com/papers/keeping-modelling-notebooks-with-trace-good-for-you-and-good-iejgo5kohb)
- [DOC] Sun et al. (2016): models should be "as simple as possible but as complicated as necessary" for predefined questions; they warn about mid-level models that mix stylised and empirical components. — [Sun et al. 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/simple-or-complicated-agent-based-models-a-complicated-issue-4izse97cw0)
- [OPINION, secondary] Course material citing Railsback & Grimm: let the modelling question filter what goes in; start simple and add complexity as confidence grows. — [Osgood lecture slides](https://www.cs.usask.ca/faculty/ndo885/Classes/MIT15879/LectureSlides/Lecture%203%20--%20Overview%20of%20Modeling%20Process%20Part%201.pdf)

**Verification by re-implementation, and its hazards**
- [DOC] Edmonds & Hales (2003): replicating a published model in two independent implementations revealed weaknesses in the original that "otherwise might not have come to light"; they conclude unreplicated simulation results cannot be trusted. — [Edmonds & Hales 2003](https://scispace.com/papers/replication-replication-and-replication-some-hard-lessons-27pvbxckla)
- [DOC] Donkin et al. (2017): replicating one complex ABM in NetLogo and Repast gave results that differed "not only in magnitude but the trends", leading to different conclusions; a written description alone was not enough, working source code was needed. — [Donkin et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/replicating-complex-agent-based-models-a-formidable-task-2nqeb3qtci)
- [DOC] Polhill et al. (2004): floating-point error plus branching on float comparisons created "ghost" agents in a published land-use model. — [Polhill et al. 2004](https://scispace.com/papers/the-ghost-in-the-model-and-other-effects-of-floating-point-3m6zp9fidh)
- [DOC] Galán et al. (2009) classify errors and artefacts by stage of ABM development and propose detection activities for each. Olsen & Raunak (2019) apply metamorphic testing as a pseudo-oracle where no true oracle exists. — [Galán et al. 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/errors-and-artefacts-in-agent-based-modelling-1ij2iifcdr); [Olsen & Raunak 2019](https://scispace.com/papers/increasing-validity-of-simulation-models-through-metamorphic-52w0masw9m)

**Cost of calibration**
- [DOC] Simulated-moments calibration needs in-the-loop simulation that "may not be feasible" for large macro ABMs; surrogate meta-models are the usual workaround. — [Barde & van der Hoog 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/an-empirical-validation-protocol-for-large-scale-agent-based-3uqiy7f1km); [Lamperti et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/agent-based-model-calibration-using-machine-learning-3bj5cihjfr)

### Inferences
- On "fix the specification before optimising": every methodology source puts purpose, patterns and conceptual model ahead of code, and treats runtime optimisation as late and optional. None recommends building computational infrastructure before the model's entities and processes are chosen [OWN].
- The Covasim trade-off is the important nuance for the engine-first side: its authors chose arrays over objects *at the start*, for performance, and paid for it in developer complexity. Data layout is an early decision even in a model-first project [OWN].
- The BeforeIT.jl history (MATLAB original, published and validated; re-implementation years later; first step for a new country was replicating the original Monte Carlo results) is a working template for "reference model, then fast engine validated against it" [OWN].
- Donkin and Polhill are the warning labels on approach (b): a second implementation written from a prose description will diverge. The mitigation is to keep the reference model executable and compare numbers, not descriptions (section 6) [OWN].

### Gaps
- How central banks and statistical offices move models from prototype to production (for example the NY Fed's DSGE port from MATLAB to Julia, FRB/US, Bank of England, OBR, microsimulation models such as EUROMOD, OpenFisca or PolicyEngine) was not researched: the search budget ran out. This is a material gap for the report.
- No quantified evidence was found on the cost of prototype-then-port in science (bug rates, effort). One search explicitly found none.
- Sargent-style verification and validation guidance, and the Axtell/Axelrod/Epstein/Cohen "docking" paper, were not retrieved.

## 4. Premature optimisation: Knuth in context, the data-oriented counter-position, and which decisions must be made early

### Takeaway
Knuth's paper argues for writing readable, correct programs first and transforming them into efficient ones where measurement shows it matters, while explicitly not passing up the critical few percent; the data-oriented camp replies that performance is a design property of data layout, not a late polish. The two positions agree more than the slogans suggest: decide the few architectural properties that are expensive to retrofit early, avoid gratuitous waste throughout, and leave real optimisation to profiles.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Knuth, "Structured Programming with go to Statements" (ACM Computing Surveys, December 1974). The abstract describes "a methodology of program design, beginning with readable and correct, but possibly inefficient programs that are systematically transformed if necessary into efficient and correct, but possibly less readable code". — [Knuth 1974](https://scispace.com/papers/structured-programming-with-go-to-statements-4ccpssngel)
- [DOC, via secondary sources] The famous sentence sits in a passage that begins "There is no doubt that the grail of efficiency leads to abuse", pairs "say about 97% of the time: premature optimization is the root of all evil" with the instruction not to pass up opportunities "in that critical 3%", and stresses measurement: it is "often a mistake to make a priori judgments about what parts of a program are really critical", because programmers' "intuitive guesses fail". — [Startifact](https://blog.startifact.com/posts/is-premature-optimization-the-root-of-all-evil/); [Facebook Engineering, The Mature Optimization Handbook](https://engineering.fb.com/2013/11/12/core-infra/the-mature-optimization-handbook/)
- [OPINION] Malte Skarupke (June 2025) re-reads the paper and argues the quote is misused to dismiss small optimisations, whereas Knuth, just before it, says small optimisations such as unrolling a loop once are worth doing in the right place. — [Revisiting Knuth's "Premature Optimization" Paper](https://probablydance.com/2025/06/19/revisiting-knuths-premature-optimization-paper/)
- [OPINION] Mike Acton, CppCon 2014 "Data-Oriented Design and C++" (slide text): "The purpose of all programs, and all parts of those programs, is to transform data from one form to another"; "If you don't understand the data you don't understand the problem"; "Different problems require different solutions. If you have different data, you have a different problem"; "Solving problems you probably don't have creates more problems you definitely do". — [Slides](https://www.slideshare.net/cellperformance/data-oriented-design-and-c); [isocpp.org](https://isocpp.org/blog/2015/01/cppcon-2014-data-oriented-design-and-c-mike-acton); review in [Overload 167](https://accu.org/journals/overload/30/167/teodorescu)
- [OPINION] Casey Muratori's three "philosophies of optimization" (as summarised by others): *optimisation* proper (tuning for specific hardware; hard, slow, only for key components); *non-pessimisation* (simply not making the computer do unnecessary work; portable, simple, applies everywhere, all the time); *fake optimisation* (context-free folklore). — [Haskell Optimization Handbook](https://haskell.foundation/hs-opt-handbook.github.io/src/Preliminaries/philosophies_of_optimization.html); [ds-rants](https://ds-rants.github.io/ds-rants/posts/2025/2025_06_05_non-pessimization/non-pessimization.html); [Pragmatic Engineer interview](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-performant-code-matters-but-gets)
- [DEV] Evidence that some things are costly to retrofit: Victoria 3's pre-release gains came from parallelising one specific calculation (modifier nodes), and its late-game cost came from pop-count growth that needed a model change after release. — [Dev Diary #76](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-76-performance); [Dev Diary #105](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-105-migration-in-1-6.1624226/page-6)
- [DOC] Non-determinism from multithreading, AI and randomness makes game bugs hard to reproduce. Floating-point comparisons can silently change simulation behaviour. — [Murphy-Hill et al. 2014](https://scispace.com/papers/cowboys-ankle-sprains-and-keepers-of-quality-how-is-video-7ew41sq3n9); [Polhill et al. 2004](https://scispace.com/papers/the-ghost-in-the-model-and-other-effects-of-floating-point-3m6zp9fidh)
- [DOC] Covasim's array-versus-object state layout was chosen up front for performance at a known cost in developer complexity. — [Kerr et al. 2021](https://scispace.com/papers/covasim-an-agent-based-model-of-covid-19-dynamics-and-92fdbabj0s)
- [DEV] Positech's Democracy 4 interpreted equation strings at run time and shipped that way; the fix (caching parsed constants) was a local change found with a profiler. — [Cliffski's blog, Dec 2021](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/12/29/speeding-up-democracy-4-simulation-processing-atof-is-slow/)

### Inferences
- Acton's own principles cut against a *generic* engine built ahead of the model: if the data define the problem, an engine written before the model's data shapes are known is "solving problems you probably don't have". They support engine-quality thinking about data the moment the shapes are known [OWN].
- **Decide early versus defer [OWN judgement, built on the findings above and section 5's measurements].**

| Decide at the walking-skeleton stage (expensive to retrofit) | Evidence | Defer until a profile demands it |
|---|---|---|
| Determinism contract: fixed time step, seeded RNG streams, fixed iteration order, defined float policy | Murphy-Hill; Polhill; replication divergence in Donkin | SIMD, cache blocking, custom allocators |
| State as flat typed arrays keyed by integer ids (not an object graph); model definition as data | Covasim trade-off; Acton; Micro-Machinations | Exact struct-of-arrays layout, f32 versus f64 per field |
| Headless core with no UI dependencies and a narrow API | Murphy-Hill (UI entanglement blocks testing) | Multithreading inside a step (but keep phases free of hidden shared mutable state so it stays possible) |
| Save/replay as serialised state plus input log, versioned from day one | Determinism findings; no direct source found (see Gaps) | Compression, incremental saves |
| Units and accounting identities enforced in types or tests | TRACE verification step | Equation JIT or bytecode compilation |
| Benchmark harness and per-step time budget in CI | Knuth (measure) | GPU compute (Positech deliberately avoided it) |

- Knuth's 1974 method ("readable and correct first, then systematically transformed") is, almost literally, the reference-implementation-then-optimised-engine workflow, with the reference kept as the correctness oracle [OWN].

### Gaps
- The original ACM text of Knuth's paper was not opened; the quotation is reconstructed from secondary sources and the indexed abstract.
- No primary Acton transcript or Muratori original was retrieved (slide text and third-party summaries only). A 2026 Muratori talk on the history of "premature optimization" appeared in results but was not read: [BSC 2026 item](https://zeli.app/story/49463888).
- Not researched (budget): Glenn Fiedler on fixed time steps and deterministic lockstep; documented cost of retrofitting multithreading in Dwarf Fortress, RimWorld or Cities: Skylines; save-format and mod-API retrofit stories. The "save/replay" and "mod API" rows above are therefore the author's judgement, not sourced findings.

## 5. Performance reality check: what a Democracy-3-sized step costs, and when a compiled engine becomes necessary

### Takeaway
A Democracy-3-sized graph (hundreds of nodes, thousands of links) costs about 10 microseconds per step in naive C or Rust, about 20 microseconds in vectorised NumPy and under a millisecond even in plain interpreted Python, so at that size language choice is irrelevant to feasibility; Democracy 4 itself shipped at roughly a microsecond per equation. A compiled, data-oriented engine becomes necessary rather than nice somewhere around a million heterogeneous entities per step, or when thousands of Monte Carlo or calibration runs of a 100,000-plus-cell model must finish in minutes.

### Cited Findings
- [DEV] Democracy 4: about 640,000 voter equations at new-game start (2,000 voters, about 10 connections each, 32 pre-processing turns); the whole pre-calculation function took 1.50 s before and 0.85 s after the `atof` fix. — [Cliffski, Nov 2022](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2022/11/05/optimization-for-fun/); [Cliffski, Dec 2021](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/12/29/speeding-up-democracy-4-simulation-processing-atof-is-slow/)
- [DOC] ABM framework benchmarks maintained by the Agents.jl developers (time relative to Agents.jl 6.2.10 = 1): WolfSheep-small: MASON 5.29, Mesa 3.2.0 9.38, NetLogo 6.4.0 9.94; WolfSheep-large: Mesa 3.28, NetLogo 4.81. Older versions showed much larger gaps (Mesa 0.8.9: 84.7x on Schelling, 120.5x on Forest Fire). The 2019 JOSS paper reported more than 8x over Mesa on forest fire. These are the framework authors' own benchmarks; the code is public. — [ABMFrameworksComparison](https://github.com/JuliaDynamics/ABMFrameworksComparison); [Agents.jl comparison page](https://juliadynamics.github.io/Agents.jl/v4.0/comparison); [JOSS paper](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.01611.pdf)
- [DOC] Covasim (Python, NumPy, Numba): realistic scenarios on a standard laptop in under a minute; compute time scales roughly linearly with agents over three orders of magnitude. — [Kerr et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8341708)
- [DOC] A comparative study of compartmental and agent-based epidemic models found run time roughly linear in simulated days and agents, and "for 50 million agents one has to wait more than 2 hours on a single node". — [arXiv 2108.11546](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.11546)
- [DOC] HPC end of the range: a full agent-based economic model with 331 million agents runs in 108 seconds on 128 CPU cores with MPI plus OpenMP (70% strong scaling). — [Gill et al. 2021](https://scispace.com/papers/high-performance-computing-implementations-of-agent-based-2tqjy37lns)
- [DOC] A US national epidemic pipeline (288 million individuals, 12.6 billion time-varying interactions) runs 400 national replicates in under 33 hours on two HPC systems. — [Bhattacharya et al. 2022](https://scispace.com/papers/data-driven-scalable-pipeline-using-national-agent-based-8y4we5bf)
- [DOC] BeforeIT.jl: orders of magnitude faster than the MATLAB original of the same macro ABM. — [BeforeIT.jl](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13267)
- [DEV] Victoria 3: four ticks per in-game day, with heavier weekly, monthly and yearly tasks; late-game slowdown tracks pop count. — [Dev Diary #76](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-76-performance); [Dev Diary #105](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-105-migration-in-1-6.1624226/page-6)

### Inferences

**Own measurement [OWN], made in this session.** A synthetic influence-graph step was implemented four ways and timed on the session sandbox (4-vCPU Intel Xeon at 2.1 GHz, single thread, gcc 13.3 `-O2`, rustc 1.97 `-C opt-level=3`, Python 3.13.18, NumPy 2.5.3; edges in random order, which is the cache-unfriendly worst case; single runs on a shared VM, so treat as order-of-magnitude). Kernel per step: `acc[dst[e]] += w[e] * v[src[e]]` over all edges, then `v[i] = 0.9*v[i] + 0.1*clamp(base[i] + acc[i], 0, 1)` over all nodes. Scripts are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-PoliSimEngineAndGame/c394c949-3bad-5f4c-9b6a-045919c86709/scratchpad/bench/` (session-scoped; re-create if needed).

| Size | C | Rust | NumPy (bincount) | Pure Python loops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 nodes, 5,000 links (Democracy-3 scale) | 8.6 µs/step (1.7 ns/link) | 12.1 µs (2.4 ns/link) | 22.5 µs (4.5 ns/link) | 727 µs (145 ns/link) |
| 100,000 nodes, 1,000,000 links | 2.9 ms | 4.7 ms | 5.3 ms | 393 ms |
| 1,000,000 nodes, 10,000,000 links | 100 ms (10 ns/link) | 113 ms | 180 ms | not run |

All four implementations produced bit-identical final state (maximum absolute difference 0.0) at every size tested. The Rust version is untuned (bounds-checked indexing); the gap to C is not a language verdict.

**Arithmetic from those figures [OWN].**
- Democracy-3 scale, one step per turn, 80 turns per game: 58 ms per whole game in pure Python, 1.8 ms in NumPy, 0.7 ms in C.
- 10,000 Monte Carlo playthroughs at that scale (800,000 steps): about 9.7 minutes in pure Python, 18 s in NumPy, 7 s in C. An AI lookahead of 50 candidate policies x 8 turns x 100 samples (40,000 steps) per decision: 29 s in pure Python (too slow for an interactive turn), 0.9 s in NumPy, 0.35 s in C.
- 100,000 cells with a million links, weekly ticks for 20 years (1,040 steps): 3 s in C, 5.6 s in NumPy, 6.8 minutes in pure Python. 1,000 Monte Carlo runs of that: 51 minutes single-threaded C, 93 minutes NumPy, 4.7 days pure Python.
- One million cells with ten million links: 0.10 to 0.18 s per step is still an acceptable end-of-turn wait, but 1,000 Monte Carlo runs of 1,040 steps is about 29 hours of single-threaded C. Per-link cost has risen from 1.7 ns to 10 ns purely from cache misses, so at this size memory layout matters more than language.
- Democracy 4's shipped figure (0.85 to 1.5 s for something of the order of 640,000 equation evaluations, taking the two blog posts together) implies roughly 1 to 2 µs per equation: about a thousand times slower per link than a flat C array kernel and slower than the pure-Python loop above, in a commercially successful C++ game. This is a rough cross-post inference, not a figure Harris states.

**Where a custom compiled engine becomes necessary [OWN].**
- Not at Democracy-3 size, even with daily ticks and thousands of calibration runs, provided the hot loop is vectorised or compiled in any way.
- NumPy stays within about 2x of naive C only while the model is a few homogeneous array kernels. It falls away when (i) every link carries its own arbitrary formula (interpretation overhead of the Democracy kind), (ii) agents branch individually, or (iii) the step is many tiny arrays (per-call overhead dominates: at 5,000 links NumPy is already 2.6x C). The Agents.jl-versus-Mesa figures (3x to 9x today, 30x to 120x historically) are the published version of the same point.
- Rough thresholds: beyond about 10^5 to 10^6 individually simulated agents with heterogeneous behaviour per tick; or beyond about 10^9 to 10^10 link-updates per user-visible wait (for example in-game forecasting fans, AI opponents planning by simulation, automated balance search); or national 1:1 populations (10^7 to 10^8 agents), which the literature handles with HPC clusters, not laptops.
- A cohort-cell design (age x sex x region x income band and similar) for a real-units population typically lands at 10^4 to 10^6 cells, which the table shows is comfortably interactive in any compiled language and tolerable in NumPy; the decision that drives engine need is cohorts versus synthetic individuals, and that is a model decision.

### Gaps
- No published per-step timing for Democracy 3 or 4's main turn was found; the microsecond-per-equation figure is inferred from two posts a year apart that may time different functions.
- The Covasim "performance comparable to C++" and BeforeIT.jl "orders of magnitude" claims are the authors' own and lack exact figures in the material retrieved; arxiv.org could not be opened to extract tables.
- No published figures were retrieved for dynamic microsimulation or tax-benefit engines, cohort-component demographic models, Paradox tick times in milliseconds, or JAX/GPU economic simulators.
- The benchmark above is a single synthetic kernel on a noisy VM with no warm-up control or repetition; it should be re-run on target hardware with the project's real step function before any threshold is treated as firm.

## 6. Rewrite risk and cost: prototype-then-rewrite versus evolve-in-place, and differential testing against a reference model

### Takeaway
Brooks withdrew "plan to throw one away" in 1995 in favour of incremental build with a system that always runs, and Spolsky's warning is about discarding a mature, bug-fixed production codebase, which a two-week reference model is not. The risk that actually applies to "prototype then engine" is silent divergence between the two implementations, and the literature's answer is differential testing with the slow model kept as an executable oracle.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] The 1995 anniversary edition of *The Mythical Man-Month* adds a chapter with sections titled "Don't Build One to Throw Away—The Waterfall Model Is Wrong!" and "An Incremental-Build Model Is Better—Progressive Refinement"; Brooks writes that "the biggest mistake in the 'Build one to throw away' concept is that it implicitly assumes the classical sequential or waterfall model". — [Book text (archive.org)](https://archive.org/stream/MythicalManMonth/Brooks%201974%20The%20Mythical%20Man-Month-%20Essays%20on%20Software%20Engineering%2C%20Anniversary%20Edition%20%282nd%20Edition%29_djvu.txt); [chapter summary](https://herbertograca.com/2018/11/19/11-plan-to-throw-one-away/)
- [OPINION] Brooks in interview: "in the second edition, I say that was misguided! You ought to plan to continually iterate on it, not just build it, throw it away and start over." — [Computerworld, The Grill: Fred Brooks](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1709141/the-grill-fred-brooks.html)
- [OPINION] Spolsky (April 2000): rewriting from scratch throws away accumulated knowledge and years of bug fixes (Netscape as the example); a slow component "affects only a small part of the project, which you can optimize or even rewrite". — [Things You Should Never Do, Part I](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/)
- [OPINION] Counter-arguments: Netscape's decline also had other causes (Microsoft's bundling, antitrust findings); Mozilla, the product of that rewrite, later succeeded; the essay assumes rewrites are driven by distaste for technical debt, which does not cover every case. — [Revisiting Spolsky](https://natashajaffe.substack.com/p/revisiting-spolskys-things-you-should?r=3v8ptd); [Netscape: the rewrite big mistake?](https://romain.bearblog.dev/netscape-the-rewrite-big-mistake/); [Lessons from six software rewrite stories (discussion)](https://news.slashdot.org/story/19/02/22/1815252/lessons-from-six-software-rewrite-stories)
- [DOC] Experience report (Pizka 2004): having tried both, adapting existing large codebases (GCC, Linux) "proved to be by far more" effective than from-scratch implementation in most cases. — [Pizka 2004](https://scispace.com/papers/adaptation-of-large-scale-open-source-software-an-experience-36if3bll13)
- [DOC] Migration experience report (Canto 2016): redeveloping from an adequate specification, rather than translating legacy code directly, significantly reduced bugs and maintenance time. — [Canto 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/migracao-de-software-consideracoes-e-experiencias-3zamlrl1ml)
- [DOC] Differential testing "uses similar programs as cross-referencing oracles to find semantic bugs that do not exhibit explicit erroneous behaviors like crashes or assertion failures". Empirical compiler-testing studies confirm its effectiveness and compare variants. — [NEZHA, Petsios et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/nezha-efficient-domain-independent-differential-testing-4hrdyt30f8); [Chen et al. 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/an-empirical-comparison-of-compiler-testing-techniques-48557l7nrd); [Lidbury et al. 2015](https://scispace.com/papers/many-core-compiler-fuzzing-10qz10pcmb)
- [DOC] Anthropic's C-compiler experiment (5 February 2026) used GCC as a "known-good oracle": random subsets of the Linux kernel were compiled with GCC and the rest with the new compiler, so that failures could be localised and agents could work in parallel. — [Anthropic Engineering: Building a C compiler with a team of parallel Claudes](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- [DOC] An LLM-driven port of a 648K-line Rust codebase to 41K lines of Python (2026 preprint) used public benchmarks as the objective, plus integration tests and explicit parity tests against the original; benchmark-driven debugging found four bugs that unit tests would not have revealed. — [Wang & Sengupta 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/from-translation-to-superset-benchmark-driven-evolution-of-a-33spx5b7cel2)
- [DOC] Independent re-implementation exposes flaws in the original (Edmonds & Hales) but re-implementation from a description alone diverges (Donkin et al.). — [Edmonds & Hales 2003](https://scispace.com/papers/replication-replication-and-replication-some-hard-lessons-27pvbxckla); [Donkin et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/replicating-complex-agent-based-models-a-formidable-task-2nqeb3qtci)

### Inferences
- Brooks's revised position supports (c) in form (always have a running system, grow it) and is compatible with a small reference model as long as the reference is not "the first system to be thrown away" but a permanent, cheap test oracle [OWN].
- Spolsky's argument has little force against replacing a days-to-weeks reference model: its value is the specification it embodies, and that is preserved as tests. It has full force against rewriting the *engine* later, which argues for getting the retrofit-expensive decisions of section 4 right at the skeleton stage [OWN].
- The combination that the evidence supports: keep the slow model runnable; generate random scenarios; require the fast engine to match within a stated tolerance (bit-exact is attainable for a fixed operation order, as the section 5 measurement showed across Python, NumPy, C and Rust); add metamorphic checks (for example, doubling all money quantities and prices leaves real quantities unchanged) where no oracle exists [OWN].
- Cost of the oracle is real but bounded: every model change must be made twice. That cost is the main argument for retiring the Python reference module by module once the engine's own golden files are trusted, or for making the reference model the *same* data-driven model definition interpreted by a naive evaluator [OWN].

### Gaps
- No rigorous empirical study of rewrite success rates was found; the evidence is essays, experience reports and case studies.
- Fowler's "strangler fig" and "sacrificial architecture" essays, McKeeman's original differential-testing paper, and industrial rewrite case studies (Dropbox's sync engine, the TypeScript compiler port) were not retrieved in this session.
- The Brooks quotations come from secondary summaries of the 1995 chapter.

## 7. Solo and small-team scope management: timelines, failure modes, early-access staging

### Takeaway
Verified timelines for comparable simulation and strategy games run from about two and a half years (an experienced team with existing technology and half the schedule spent in Early Access) to six to nine years (solo or small teams building their own technology); scope, not technology, is the most frequently reported failure. Verified data for the specific political sims named in the brief could not be collected in this session.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Scope is the dominant reported problem: unrealistic scope and feature creep each in 75% of 20 postmortems; features cut in 70%; technology problems in 60%. — [Petrillo et al. 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/what-went-wrong-a-survey-of-problems-in-game-development-3m7iflo7ak)

### Inferences
- For a solo developer building custom technology, the verified comparables (Songs of Syx, Noita, Factorio's early years) imply planning for several years to a public playable and treating anything that delays the first playable as the main schedule risk [OWN].
- The staging pattern common to OTC, Factorio, Songs of Syx and Terra Invicta is: small playable core, then public or semi-public iteration for a large fraction of the project, with systems added in layers. None staged it as "engine complete, then game" [OWN].
- With 75% of projects reporting scope problems, each roadmap milestone needs an explicit "not in this milestone" list and a time box; the engine is the easiest place for a programmer-owner to let scope grow unobserved, because engine work produces visible progress without testing the game hypothesis (Delay's "gaping hole") [OWN].

## 8. AI-assisted development in 2025–2026: Rust versus other languages, and what makes AI-written code reliable

### Takeaway
The best evidence says AI coding help is real but conditional: the one randomised trial found experienced developers slower with early-2025 tools, later data point to speed-ups but are confounded, and the most impressive agent result (a 100,000-line Rust compiler) depended on a near-perfect test oracle and still hit a quality ceiling. For this project the implication is that the reference model, golden tests and benchmarks are not overhead: they are what makes AI-written engine code trustworthy, and they have to exist before the bulk of the engine is generated.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] METR randomised controlled trial (16 experienced open-source developers, 246 tasks in mature repositories, February to June 2025 tools, mainly Cursor Pro): tasks took 19% longer with AI allowed (confidence interval +2% to +39%); developers had predicted a 24% speed-up and afterwards still believed they had been about 20% faster. — [METR, 10 July 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/); [Becker et al. 2025](https://scispace.com/papers/measuring-the-impact-of-early-2025-ai-on-experienced-open-o2yurz1hz0t5)
- [DOC] METR update (24 February 2026): in a late-2025 follow-up the estimated change in task time was -18% for returning developers (interval -38% to +9%) and -4% for newly recruited developers (interval -15% to +9%); both intervals include zero. METR says the design now suffers selection effects (30 to 50% of developers reported not submitting tasks they did not want to do without AI), believes developers are probably more sped up in early 2026 than in early 2025, calls its data "only very weak evidence" for the size, and is redesigning the study. Sign convention should be re-checked at source; the page could not be opened. — [METR, 24 Feb 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/); commentary warning against over-reading it: [Rob Bowley, April 2026](https://blog.robbowley.net/2026/04/04/metrs-developer-productivity-research-2026-update/)
- [DOC] Anthropic, 5 February 2026 (Nicholas Carlini): 16 parallel Claude Opus 4.6 agents, nearly 2,000 sessions over two weeks, just under $20,000 of API cost, produced a roughly 100,000-line C compiler in Rust (standard library only) that builds Linux 6.9 on three architectures and passes about 99% of the GCC torture tests. Stated lessons: "the task verifier is nearly perfect, otherwise Claude will solve the wrong problem"; keep test output terse and greppable; give a fast sampled test mode because the model "can't tell time"; use an existing compiler as oracle to split a monolithic task; run CI so new commits cannot break old behaviour; use specialised agent roles. Stated limits: generated code is less efficient than GCC with optimisation off; Rust quality is "reasonable" but well short of an expert's; new features and fixes frequently broke existing functionality; the project "has nearly reached the limits of Opus's abilities". — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)
- [OPINION] Sceptical coverage: the compiler leaned on existing open-source test suites and substantial human scaffolding. — [The Register, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/13/anthropic_c_compiler)
- [DOC] Multi-SWE-bench (NeurIPS 2025 datasets track): issue-resolution rates fall sharply outside Python; with one agent framework Claude 3.7 Sonnet resolved 45.8% of Python issues but 6.7% of Rust issues, and the best agent reached about 12% on Rust; C and C++ were also low. — [Zan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02605)
- [DOC, conflicting] SWE-bench Multilingual (a different benchmark) reports Rust with the *highest* resolution rate among its languages and C/C++ the lowest. The two benchmarks disagree on Rust's relative standing. — [SWE-bench Multilingual](https://swebench.com/multilingual)
- [DOC] Rust-SWE-bench (500 tasks, 34 repositories, 2026): ReAct-style agents resolved up to 21.2%; the best system (with Claude 3.7 Sonnet) 28.6%. The two main failure causes were difficulty comprehending repository-wide structure and poor compliance with Rust's strict type and trait semantics; reliable issue reproduction (an automated test environment) was critical. — [Xiang et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/evaluating-and-improving-automated-repository-level-rust-01x3iy175hns)
- [DOC] A 2026 survey concludes LLM code research remains biased to high-resource languages "with weaker performance in languages like Rust and OCaml". — [Jiang et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/large-language-models-for-multilingual-code-intelligence-a-gayllo71uaya)
- [DOC] Tests and specifications as inputs: giving GPT-4 the tests along with the problem solves more problems than the problem alone (MBPP, HumanEval, CodeChef). A 2026 preprint finds that grounding the *test writer* in an explicit specification raised correct-code rates by 38 percentage points across three Claude tiers (36 on a held-out set), while doubling the number of ungrounded tests barely helped; without the spec the tester found 2 of 30 seeded bugs, with it 27 of 30. Embedding assertions in prompts gave 20 to 30 point gains for some open models. — [Mathews & Nagappan 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/test-driven-development-for-code-generation-29rqzbfrd2); [Haeri & Ghelichi 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/specification-grounding-drives-test-effectiveness-for-llm-sz3q2t056xin); [Korraprolu et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/prompt-assert-repeat-test-driven-prompting-as-a-quality-btr7ikgpez73)
- [DOC] LLM-assisted cross-language porting at scale is now reported as practical when driven by behavioural benchmarks and parity tests (648K lines of Rust to 41K lines of Python at near parity). — [Wang & Sengupta 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/from-translation-to-superset-benchmark-driven-evolution-of-a-33spx5b7cel2)
- [DOC] Survey synthesis (2026): adoption is rising while trust in AI output accuracy is falling. — [Colafranceschi 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/the-status-of-artificial-intelligence-in-the-developer-b3foyfcsfig0)

### Inferences
- The Rust benchmark numbers come from 2025-generation models on *other people's large repositories*; a greenfield engine with small modules and strong tests is a much friendlier setting, and Carlini's result shows 2026-generation agents can sustain a 100,000-line Rust codebase when an oracle exists. But the same result shows regressions and inefficiency at the limit, so performance-critical kernels still need human-owned benchmarks [OWN].
- Rust's compiler acts as an additional automatic verifier for agent-written code (type and ownership errors are caught before tests run), which is consistent with Rust-SWE-bench's finding that type and trait compliance is where agents struggle: the struggle surfaces as compile errors rather than latent bugs. No head-to-head evidence on Rust versus C or Zig for AI-written numerical code was found; this point is reasoning, not data [OWN].
- AI assistance lowers the cost of both the engine and of a later port, so it weakens the "we must pick the final architecture now" argument as much as it weakens the "an engine is too expensive to build early" argument. What it does not lower is the cost of *knowing what the model should compute*; the specification-grounding result says that is the binding constraint on AI code correctness [OWN].
- Implication for build order: (1) written spec and executable reference model first, because they are the "nearly perfect verifier"; (2) golden and differential tests wired into CI before bulk engine generation; (3) small modules with typed interfaces; (4) benchmarks with budgets, because agents will not notice a 10x slowdown; (5) expect METR-style over-estimation of one's own speed-up and measure milestones by exit criteria, not by lines produced [OWN].

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence was found comparing AI-assistant effectiveness across Rust, C, C++ and Zig specifically for systems or numerical code. Aider's polyglot benchmark, the 2025 DORA and Stack Overflow results, and reports of agent-built large Rust projects other than Carlini's were not retrieved.
- METR's May 2026 report on self-reported productivity and its later 2026 work were seen in an index only and not read: [METR research index](https://metr.org/research/).
- Benchmarks age quickly; the models named in the newest papers (for example Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.3-codex) post-date the Rust-specific benchmarks, so current Rust capability is probably understated by the figures above. That is an inference, not a measurement.

## 9. Risk register for this project: which order of work retires the biggest risks earliest

### Takeaway
Ranked by the evidence gathered, model-and-fun risk and scope risk are the most likely project killers, and performance risk is the least likely at Democracy scale; engine-first retires the smallest risk first and the largest last. A short reference-model phase followed immediately by a walking skeleton retires model, tooling and determinism risk within weeks and leaves performance as a measured quantity rather than a fear.

### Cited Findings
- [DEV] Model/fun risk materialised as six lost years in Subversion ("massive gaping hole" where the game should be). — [MCV/Develop](https://www.mcvuk.com/subversion-dropped-after-six-years-development/)
- [DOC] Scope risk is the most frequently reported failure (75% of postmortems), ahead of technology (60%). — [Petrillo et al. 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/what-went-wrong-a-survey-of-problems-in-game-development-3m7iflo7ak)
- [DOC] Believability is hard to validate even for the genre leader ("incredibly hard to research"). — [PC Gamer on Democracy 3](https://pcgamer.com/democracy-3-hands-on-losing-elections-with-land-mines-in-a-complex-politics-sim/2)
- [DOC] Calibration of large economic ABMs is computationally and methodologically hard; simulated-moments estimation may be infeasible without surrogates. — [Barde & van der Hoog 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/an-empirical-validation-protocol-for-large-scale-agent-based-3uqiy7f1km)
- [DEV] Performance risk in this genre has been handled after the fact with a profiler (Democracy 4) and by changing model entity counts (Victoria 3). — [Cliffski, Dec 2021](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/12/29/speeding-up-democracy-4-simulation-processing-atof-is-slow/); [Victoria 3 Dev Diary #105](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-105-migration-in-1-6.1624226/page-6)
- [DEV] UI and rendering, not simulation, produced Democracy 4's visible frame-rate problems (19 to 109 fps and 59 to 228 fps fixes). — [Cliffski, Mar 2023](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2023/03/15/speeding-up-my-game-from-59fps-to-228-fps/)
- [DOC] Tooling/AI risk: agent-written Rust is feasible with a strong oracle but regresses without one; experienced developers mis-estimate AI speed-up. — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler); [METR 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)
- [DOC] Divergence risk between two implementations is real when the second is written from a description. — [Donkin et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/replicating-complex-agent-based-models-a-formidable-task-2nqeb3qtci)

### Inferences
All ratings below are the researcher's judgement [OWN], anchored to the findings cited above and in earlier sections.

| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Earliest retirement under (a) engine first | Under (b) model first | Under (c) vertical slice | Under hybrid: time-boxed reference model, then walking skeleton |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model risk: economy not believable, unstable or not legible | High / fatal | Late (after engine) | Weeks | Weeks to months (slower iteration) | Weeks |
| Fun risk: believable but not enjoyable to steer | High / fatal | Last | Not retired (no game) | First playable | First playable, slightly later than (c) |
| Scope risk | High / high | Worsened (engine scope is unbounded) | Moderate (model can sprawl) | Best contained (everything must fit the slice) | Contained if milestones carry exclusion lists |
| Calibration/data risk for one real country | Medium-high / high | Late | Early, cheaply (notebooks) | Mid | Early (reference model doubles as data sandbox) |
| Performance risk at chosen model size | Low at Democracy scale; medium if agents or heavy Monte Carlo | First | Unknown until port | Measured continuously | Measured from skeleton onwards with budgets |
| Retrofit risk: determinism, layout, save format | Medium / high | Addressed early but against guessed requirements | Deferred, may bite at port | Addressed early against a real, tiny model | Addressed at skeleton against a real, tiny model |
| Divergence risk between prototype and engine | n/a | n/a | High unless oracle-tested | n/a | Controlled by differential tests |
| Tooling/AI risk: unreliable generated code, weak Rust results | Medium / medium | High (large codebase before any oracle) | Low (Python is agents' strongest language) | Medium | Low-medium (oracle exists before bulk Rust) |
| UI risk: information-dense strategy UI is hard | Medium / high | Last | Not addressed | Early, cheaply | Early, cheaply |
| Motivation/continuity risk for a solo owner | Unknown | Owner prefers this order (positive) | Unknown | Playable feedback (positive) | Mixed; engine-quality work begins within weeks |

- The order that retires the most probability-weighted risk earliest is the hybrid: (b) as a strictly time-boxed first step that produces an executable specification, then (c) with the engine core built to engine standards from the first commit.
- The project owner's instinct is right about *what* must not be bolted on later (deterministic, data-oriented, fast core) and about personal motivation; the evidence disagrees only on *when* the bulk of that work should happen and on how much of it Democracy-scale models need.
- Engine-first becomes the rational choice if the design commits, before any modelling, to simulating individuals at 10^6-plus scale or to simulation-in-the-loop AI and forecasting on every turn. That commitment should be made explicitly, with the section 5 arithmetic in hand, rather than assumed.

### Gaps
- Likelihood ratings are judgements; no base-rate data exist for this genre. No evidence was collected on solo-developer motivation effects of each order.
- Data and licensing risk for real-country statistics, and political-sensitivity or reputational risk of modelling real politics, were out of scope for this note.

## 10. Concrete roadmap patterns: milestones, ordering evidence and exit criteria

### Takeaway
The milestone list suggested in the brief (M0 spec and tiny reference model through M6 calibration harness) matches what the modelling-methodology and incremental-build literature recommend, with one change the game-development evidence argues for: something playable by outsiders must exist well before the proposed M4. Exit criteria should be numeric tests (identities, golden files, benchmarks, playtest completions), because both the AI-agent and the modelling literature make the verifier the critical asset.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Order "objectives, conceptual model, implementation and verification, sensitivity, validation" is the TRACE modelling cycle; ODD can serve as the design workflow; POM supplies the acceptance patterns. — [Schmolke et al. 2010](https://scispace.com/papers/ecological-models-supporting-environmental-decision-making-a-2kyjmkcd1b); [Grimm et al. 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/the-odd-protocol-for-describing-agent-based-and-other-1zkv2up97k); [Grimm & Railsback 2012](https://scispace.com/papers/pattern-oriented-modelling-a-multi-scope-for-predictive-33wbsytap2)
- [DOC] A game economy can be simulated and balanced "before [it is] built". — [Dormans 2011](https://scispace.com/papers/simulating-mechanics-to-study-emergence-in-games-v7dak13j0y)
- [OPINION] Walking skeleton: thinnest end-to-end slice linking the main components, kept and grown. Brooks (1995): incremental build with progressive refinement. — [Xebia](https://xebia.com/blog/the-technical-walking-skeleton/); [Brooks, anniversary edition](https://archive.org/stream/MythicalManMonth/Brooks%201974%20The%20Mythical%20Man-Month-%20Essays%20on%20Software%20Engineering%2C%20Anniversary%20Edition%20%282nd%20Edition%29_djvu.txt)
- [DOC] Agent-built code needs a near-perfect verifier, CI against regressions and an oracle; spec-grounded tests matter more than test count. — [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler); [Haeri & Ghelichi 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/specification-grounding-drives-test-effectiveness-for-llm-sz3q2t056xin)
- [DEV] Wube invests in tests as the way to move fast. — [FFF #366 thread](https://forums.factorio.com/98893)
- [DOC] New-country adaptation of a macro ABM began by replicating the original's results, then calibrating. — [Di Domenico et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/a-large-scale-data-driven-agent-based-model-for-the-italian-2jpoaqwrks)
- [DEV] OTC put a prototype in outside hands early and spent about half the project iterating in Early Access. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/offworld-trading-company-prototype-is-now-available-as-a-founders-elite-edition-preorder-bonus)
- [DEV] Democracy 4's political layer is about 2,000 voters with about 10 connections each. — [Cliffski, Nov 2022](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2022/11/05/optimization-for-fun/)
- [DOC] Metamorphic testing gives pseudo-oracles for simulations with no ground truth; surrogates cut calibration cost. — [Olsen & Raunak 2019](https://scispace.com/papers/increasing-validity-of-simulation-models-through-metamorphic-52w0masw9m); [Lamperti et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/agent-based-model-calibration-using-machine-learning-3bj5cihjfr)
- [DOC] Prototypes helped teams fix resources and schedules and became the production base; a clear concept before production is the most-cited success factor. — [Washburn et al. 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/what-went-right-and-what-went-wrong-an-analysis-of-155-30e5mpqcdd)

### Inferences
The roadmap below is a proposal [OWN] assembled from the evidence; durations are indicative for one person with AI assistance and are not sourced.

| Milestone | Content | Evidence for placing it here | Exit criteria (all testable) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M0. Spec and tiny reference model** (hard time box, 2 to 4 weeks) | ODD-style written spec (purpose, entities, state variables with units, schedule, submodels); list of 5 to 10 target patterns (for example debt-dynamics identity, plausible unemployment-inflation co-movement, cohort ageing); Python/NumPy reference of a few dozen nodes plus one cohort block | TRACE/ODD/POM order; Machinations; Boehm (45% less effort); Covasim built in weeks; AI needs the verifier first | Accounting identities hold to 1e-9 over 200 steps; every target pattern reproduced qualitatively; a person can make meaningful choices for 20 turns from a CLI or notebook; decision recorded on cohorts versus individuals with the section 5 arithmetic |
| **M1. Walking skeleton: core, CLI, golden tests** | Core library in the final language: deterministic fixed-step loop, seeded RNG, flat typed state, model loaded from data files, save/load, replay log; CLI that plays a turn; differential tests against M0; CI; first benchmark with a stated step budget | Cockburn; Brooks 1995; Carlini (oracle, CI); Edmonds & Hales; section 4 retrofit list; section 5 bit-identical result | Engine matches reference on N random scenarios within stated tolerance; save, load and continue equals an uninterrupted run; same seed gives identical results on two operating systems; benchmark recorded and budget met at 10x current model size; a text-mode game is playable end to end |
| **M2. Population module and benchmarks** | Real-units demography and labour-force cohorts; synthetic scale tests; profile; decide float width and threading plan only if the budget is missed | Knuth (measure, then transform); Acton (data shapes now known); Victoria 3 pop-count lesson | End-of-turn time and K-run Monte Carlo time within budget at target cell count; entity count provably bounded over a 50-year run (no pop fragmentation); memory budget met |
| **M3. Scenario data pipeline for one country** | Reproducible ETL from official statistics to model inputs; baseline calibration; hindcast | Poledna-lineage practice (replicate, then calibrate); TRACE validation on independent data | Baseline within tolerance bands on headline aggregates over a 10 to 20 year hindcast; provenance of every input documented; rebuild from raw data is one command |
| **M4. Client prototype** | Cheap graphical client over the core API; first external playtests | OTC feedback loop; Subversion lesson; Murphy-Hill (keep UI separate) | N outside players finish a term unaided; each can explain one causal chain the sim produced; top confusions logged |
| **M5. Political layer** | Voter groups, parties, elections, events on top of cohorts | Democracy's shipped scale as a sanity bound | Elections respond to policy in the direction of the design patterns; no dominant trivial strategy found by scripted bots |
| **M6. Calibration and balancing harness** | Monte Carlo runner, sensitivity analysis, metamorphic tests, bot players, balance regression suite, optional surrogate | Lamperti; Olsen & Raunak; Factorio's test culture | No divergent or NaN runs in 10,000 random-policy games; sensitivity ranking stable; balance metrics tracked in CI |

- **Recommended change to the brief's ordering [OWN].** Pull a playable build forward: the M1 CLI is the first playable, and at least one outsider playtest of a text or minimal UI should happen by the end of M2. Under the brief's order the first test of "is this fun?" arrives at M4, after data-pipeline work whose value depends on the answer. Consider swapping M3 and M4, or running M4 in parallel with M2 against a stylised country.
- **How this accommodates the engine-first view [OWN].** From M1 the core *is* the engine: Rust (or C), deterministic, data-oriented, benchmarked on every commit. What is deferred is breadth (generic graph features, threading, SIMD, GPU) until the reference model and the profiles ask for it. A protected engine-optimisation slot after M2, entered only with a profile in hand, gives the owner's interest a place in the plan with a measurable goal.
- **How this accommodates the model-first view [OWN].** M0 is model-first, but time-boxed and with a hand-off contract (the differential tests), which addresses the two documented failure modes of (b): no game, and silent divergence.
- **Kill and pivot criteria worth stating up front [OWN].** If M0 overruns its box twice, the model is too ambitious for the milestone, not the tooling. If M1 cannot match the reference, fix determinism before adding features. If the M2 budget is missed by more than 10x after one profiling pass, revisit cohorts versus agents before optimising code.

### Gaps
- No published milestone plans or exit criteria from comparable commercial projects (Positech, Paradox, Wube, Mohawk) were found; the exit criteria above are constructed from methodology literature, not copied from a comparable game's process.
- The proposed durations have no empirical basis in the sources.
- Whether the reference model should be retired after M2 or kept for the life of the project is unresolved by the evidence; the double-maintenance cost was not quantified anywhere.
