## 1. System dynamics / causal-loop models, and how Democracy 3's graph relates to them

### Takeaway
System dynamics (SD) is a small-state, cheap-to-integrate paradigm (tens to a few hundred equations, fixed-step Euler) that is proven in interactive policy simulators (En-ROADS runs a century in under a second in a browser). Democracy 3's weighted effect graph is best understood as a unit-less, accounting-free cousin of SD; moving to real units means adding conserved stocks and accounting identities, which is exactly what SFC models add (section 2).

### Cited Findings
- SD models are ODE systems normally stepped with fixed-step Euler: STELLA "just like the older Dynamo software, uses by default a fixed-step Euler algorithm" — [isee systems, "How model equations are solved"](https://iseesystems.com/Resources/Help/v3/Content/08-Reference/05-Computational_Details/How_model_equations_are_solved.htm)
- PySD (open-source Python runner for Vensim/XMILE models): older docs say the model is passed to SciPy's `odeint`; newer v3 docs say a forward-Euler solver executes the generated model — [PySD docs, structure](https://pysd.readthedocs.io/en/lastpy2/development/structure.html)
- World3 size is small, but sources disagree on the count: a TypeScript port reports "13 stocks" and "88 equations mapped to DYNAMO source" — [serroba/world3](https://github.com/serroba/world3); a Modelica reimplementation describes 12 state variables, rising to 29th order once internal delay functions are counted — [Cellier, Modelica 2008](https://modelica.org/events/conference2008/sessions/session4a2.pdf)
- PyWorld3 states that World3 is "an Ordinary Differential Equation solved by a Backward Euler method", with time limits and step set at instantiation — [mBarreau/pyworld3](https://github.com/mBarreau/pyworld3). Other open implementations exist in Julia — [WorldDynamics.jl](https://worlddynamics.github.io/WorldDynamics.jl/dev/world3)
- En-ROADS (Climate Interactive / MIT Sloan) is a non-linear differential-equation model built in Vensim, translated to WebAssembly with SDEverywhere so it runs in a browser; it exposes roughly 250 parameters and 180 graphs — [EGU21 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-7608.html), [Climate Interactive, simulator science](https://www.climateinteractive.org/simulators-science/)
- En-ROADS is described as simulating 100 years of energy, land and climate data "in less than a second" (secondary report, not a benchmark) — [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/node/22972)
- SD has been used for whole-country planning: the Millennium Institute's Threshold 21 (T21) integrated national development model has been customised to more than a dozen countries — [Barney 2002, System Dynamics Review](https://scispace.com/papers/the-global-2000-report-to-the-president-and-the-threshold-21-36eppxe727); T21 / iSDG applications for Swaziland, Ivory Coast, Jamaica — [Pedercini et al. 2019](https://scispace.com/papers/integrated-simulation-for-national-development-planning-q8v0rlpikb), [Pedercini et al. 2018](https://scispace.com/papers/toward-achieving-sustainable-development-goals-in-ivory-2bytpf0py6); a state-level macro-planning SD model for Sarawak — [Dangerfield 2006](https://scispace.com/papers/a-system-dynamics-model-for-economic-planning-in-sarawak-1vu8slzug9)
- Democracy 3: Positech describes "a custom-designed neural network" modelling individual voters with varying memberships of voting groups, parties and pressure groups; each voter's income, complacency and cynicism are modelled; it also models "the global economy, including credit rating agencies and debt interest levels" — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/245470/Democracy_3)
- Reviewers describe the design as a network in which "every system feeds back into every other, to a greater or lesser degree" — [New Statesman, 2013](https://www.newstatesman.com/culture/2013/09/democracy-3-simulating-government-post-recession-world); and note a system of overrides so players/modders can "customise every policy and every equation" — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/democracy-3-hands-on-losing-elections-with-land-mines-in-a-complex-politics-sim/2)
- The Godley–Lavoie GROWTH model (an SFC model) has been implemented directly in an SD tool (Insight Maker) — [Insight Maker](https://insightmaker.com/insight/185550/Godley-and-Lavoie-Growth-Model)

### Inferences
- Democracy 3's simulation is a directed weighted graph of normalised nodes with lagged effects. That is an executable causal-loop diagram: it has SD's feedback structure but none of SD's stock–flow bookkeeping, no units and no conservation laws. Nothing in it prevents money appearing from nowhere, which is harmless at 0–1 scale and fatal once GDP, debt and employment carry real units.
- For a game, SD's strengths are: tiny state, deterministic, trivially fast, easy to visualise as cause-and-effect arrows (the Democracy UI metaphor carries over), and mature tooling for sensitivity sweeps. Weaknesses: aggregate only (no distribution of households unless added), parameters are usually set by judgement and table functions, and accounting consistency is optional and easy to violate.
- The Insight Maker port of GROWTH shows that SD and SFC are computationally the same kind of object (a few hundred difference equations per period). The practical choice is therefore "SD with enforced accounting" rather than SD versus SFC.
- Researcher micro-benchmark: a dense fixed-point sweep over 500 simultaneous linear equations converged in about 3 ms per period in NumPy and about 200 ms in naive pure Python; a compiled game engine would sit at or below the NumPy figure. SD/SFC-scale systems are far inside a sub-second turn budget.

### Gaps
- Positech's modding documentation (the authoritative description of the Democracy 3/4 effect syntax) could not be fetched (`positech.co.uk` blocked). My recollection, to verify at [positech.co.uk/democracy4/modding.html](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/modding.html): simulation values are 0–1, effects are CSV entries of the form `Target, a+(b*x), inertia`, one turn is a quarter, and "situations" trigger on thresholds.
- I found no primary source confirming Forrester-era critiques of SD for macroeconomics (e.g. Nordhaus's "measurement without data"); treat that critique as unverified here.
- No verified equation count for En-ROADS, T21 or iSDG.
- Minsky (Steve Keen's open-source SD tool with "Godley tables") is a likely relevant bridge between SD and SFC; not verified this session.

## 2. Stock-flow consistent (SFC) macro models

### Takeaway
SFC models are sets of a few dozen to a few hundred difference equations built on balance-sheet and transaction-flow matrices; they solve per period with Gauss-Seidel, Broyden or Newton on the simultaneous block. They are the cheapest way to get real-unit accounting integrity (every flow has a source and destination, every deficit accumulates into someone's debt), and several open tools and empirical country models exist, though the theory-of-behaviour layer is contested and usually demand-led.

### Cited Findings
- SFC modelling follows Godley and Tobin (1970s) and was systematised by Godley & Lavoie, *Monetary Economics* (2007); the survey of record is Nikiforos & Zezza (Journal of Economic Surveys, 2017) — [SciSpace record](https://scispace.com/papers/stock-flow-consistent-macroeconomic-models-a-survey-1q6fttnuw1), [Levy WP 891](https://levyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/wp_891.pdf)
- Four accounting principles define the approach: flow consistency, stock consistency, stock-flow consistency and quadruple book-keeping; identities derived from them are coupled with behavioural equations — [Carnevali et al. 2019](https://scispace.com/papers/stock-flow-consistent-dynamic-models-features-limitations-1t6xg165oj)
- The accounting framework "imposes certain constraints and reduces the degrees of freedom of the model"; SFC models typically have a post-Keynesian, demand-led closure in which full employment is not the normal state — [Nikiforos & Zezza, Levy WP 891](https://levyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/wp_891.pdf)
- Godley–Lavoie convention: each endogenous variable appears once on the left-hand side, which makes counting equations and unknowns easy — [Lavoie & Godley, Levy WP 302](https://levyinstitute.org/pubs/wp302.pdf). The GROWTH model is chapter 11 (pp. 378–444) of the 2007 book — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1057/9780230626546_11)
- `sfcr` (R, CRAN): writes, simulates and validates SFC models; three solvers for cyclical blocks (Gauss-Seidel, Broyden, Newton-Raphson), Broyden is the default and described as usually fastest; solvers run only on blocks of mutually dependent equations found by depth-first search; CRAN 0.2.1 (Oct 2022), r-universe lists 0.2.3 (9 Oct 2025) — [sfcr manual](https://cran.r-universe.dev/sfcr/doc/manual.html), [CRAN PDF](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/packages/sfcr/sfcr.pdf). It ships a replication of GROWTH — [sfcr GROWTH vignette](https://rdrr.io/github/joaomacalos/sfcr/f/vignettes/articles/gl8-growth.Rmd)
- `pysolve` (Python, v0.2.0): equation-system solver originally motivated by SFC models, with Godley–Lavoie examples in a companion repository — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pysolve/)
- `sfctools` (Python, German Aerospace Center DLR): toolbox for SFC agent-based models with a GUI ("Attune"), published in JOSS 2023 — [JOSS paper](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.04980.pdf), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/sfctools/1.0.3/)
- `sfc-models` (Python, Brian Romanchuk): SFC model generation, Apache 2.0 — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/sfc-models/1.0.3)
- Bank of England Staff WP 614 (Burgess, Burrows, Godin, Kinsella, Millard, Sept 2016): seven sectors (households, firms, government, banks, central bank, insurance companies & pension funds, rest of world); built on ONS data from 1987; developed, estimated and calibrated "from first principles" with a purpose-built stock-flow coherent database; used as a medium-term scenario tool (e.g. higher bank capital ratios) — [BoE page](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2016/a-dynamic-model-of-financial-balances-for-the-uk), [SciSpace abstract](https://scispace.com/papers/a-dynamic-model-of-financial-balances-for-the-united-kingdom-walmqy3hh6), [Bank Underground](https://bankunderground.co.uk/2016/10/26/a-dynamic-model-of-financial-balances-for-the-united-kingdom/)
- Other empirical country SFC models: Denmark, QMDE (Byrialsen, Raza, Valdecantos; quarterly, estimated on 2005–2020 data) — [FMM WP 79/2022](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/510735086/p_fmm_imk_wp_79_2022.pdf); Italy (Zezza & Zezza, Levy WP 958, 2020, quarterly) — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/lev/wrkpap/wp_958.html); UK, UKFINMOD (Lu, Review of Income and Wealth 2025) — [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/household-debt-financial-balance-and-macroeconomic-stability-63u0biwvogtz); a two-household UK SFC model (Review of Political Economy, 2026) — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09538259.2026.2691595)
- SFC + input-output has been done: Berg, Hartley & Richters (2015) give a general SFC input-output model with an arbitrary number of sectors — [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/a-stock-flow-consistent-input-output-model-with-applications-1mva54buee)
- COMPASS (2026): an open-source ecological macro model built on an SFC core, implemented in Julia, with scenario levers including working-time reduction, universal basic income, more progressive taxation, renewable subsidies and carbon tax — [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/compass-an-ecological-macroeconomic-model-for-planetary-and-347h87ihcqp0)
- A 2026 survey reviews 58 SFC models applied to sustainability — [Llases et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/stock-flow-consistent-models-and-sustainability-a-survey-ypopoa11rc7w)

### Inferences
- What accounting consistency buys a game: (1) the budget deficit, private saving and the current account are forced to add up, so player exploits of the "free money" kind are structurally impossible; (2) debt, interest and wealth dynamics emerge from the bookkeeping instead of being hand-tuned; (3) a built-in test oracle, because the redundant equation (the one identity implied by all the others) must hold to machine precision every turn, which is ideal for automated headless regression tests; (4) initial values come straight from national sector accounts and flow-of-funds tables.
- Suitability for a game is high on cost and explainability (a transaction-flow matrix is literally a table the player can inspect) and moderate on behaviour: SFC tells you where money goes, not why agents act, so every behavioural equation (consumption, investment, portfolio choice, wage/price setting) is still a design decision with contested parameters.
- Solver risk is the main engineering concern. Iterative within-period solvers can fail to converge after an extreme player action. A game can sidestep this by lagging enough variables to make each period fully recursive (evaluate in dependency order, no iteration), accepting one-period delays, or by keeping a small simultaneous core with a guaranteed-contraction structure.
- Demand-led closure means that, without an explicit supply side (capacity, labour constraint, inflation response), stimulus always "works". A game needs a supply block bolted on (Phillips-curve wage–price block, capacity utilisation, potential output) to avoid a dominant strategy.

### Gaps
- Equation counts: not confirmed for BoE WP 614, QMDE or the Italian model. My recollection (verify): Godley–Lavoie's Model SIM has 11 equations and GROWTH roughly 90–100; empirical country models run to several hundred.
- Licences for `pysolve` and `sfctools` not confirmed (check repository LICENSE files). The "PK-SFC" collection of R models was not found by search.
- No published per-period solve times for SFC models were found; see the micro-benchmark in section 1 for an order of magnitude.

## 3. Input-output, SAM and CGE models

### Takeaway
A Leontief/IO layer of roughly 45–64 industries is cheap, well supplied with open data (FIGARO, OECD ICIO, WIOD) and adds supply chains, sectoral employment and import leakage; full CGE (GTAP/ORANI-style) is a poor fit because its best data and solvers are proprietary and solving a nonlinear market-clearing system every turn buys equilibrium assumptions a game does not need. The practical game form is a dynamic, non-equilibrium IO model with partial adjustment.

### Cited Findings
- Eurostat/JRC FIGARO: inter-country supply, use and input-output tables (product-by-product and industry-by-industry) — [Eurostat SUIOT data page](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/esa-supply-use-input-tables/information-data); the 2022 edition covers the 27 EU states plus main trading partners and a rest-of-world region — [FIGARO 2022 edition presentation](https://www.cepal.org/sites/default/files/presentations/2._eu_ic_sut_and_iot_-_2022_figaro_edition.pdf); a 2025 edition and a FIGARO-NAM (multi-country national accounting matrices, i.e. SAM-like) 2025 edition exist — [Eurostat FIGARO-NAM 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-statistical-working-papers/w/ks-01-25-050)
- FIGARO has been used at "46-country, 64-sector" resolution to initialise a multi-country agent-based model — [DEPLOYERS, Jaraíz & Pinacho 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/deployers-an-agent-based-modeling-tool-for-multi-country-3d62jhfl4q1i)
- OECD ICIO: the 2025 edition covers 1995–2022 and expanded from 76 to 80 economies — [OECD blog, Nov 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/blogs/2025/11/oecd-inter-country-input-output-icio-tables-and-trade-in-value-added-tiva-database-launch-of-the-2025-edition.html), [IIOA Newsletter 62](https://www.iioa.org/news/newsletters/Newsletter62.pdf); an earlier edition has 45 industries and a newer OECD page describes 80 economies plus rest-of-world and 50 industries for 1995–2024 — [OECD ICIO page](https://t4.oecd.org/sti/ind/inter-country-input-output-tables.htm)
- EXIOBASE 3: rectangular supply-use tables at 163 industries by 200 products; free under CC BY-SA 4.0, with a planned move to non-commercial/dual licensing from version 3.9 — [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/5589597), [exiobase site](https://exiobase.iedl.no)
- WIOD 2016 release: 43 countries, 56 sectors (ISIC Rev. 4), CC BY 4.0 — [GGDC](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/blog/blog-08-11-2016-release-of-new-wiod-data)
- GTAP: the GTAP 11 Data Base is no longer sold; GTAP 12 lists a new private-sector (for-profit) price of US$10,000 for high-income economies — [GTAP pricing](https://gtap.agecon.purdue.edu/databases/pricing.asp), [GTAP 11 page](https://www.gtap.agecon.purdue.edu/databases/v11/). Running larger GTAP aggregations needs a GEMPACK large-simulation licence (about US$300) — [RunGTAP help](https://copsmodels.com/webhelp/rungtap/hc_licence.htm)
- Open CGE tooling: MPSGE.jl is a free, open-source Julia package for building CGE models from tabular definitions — [MPSGE.jl docs](https://julia-mpsge.github.io/MPSGE.jl/dev/), [JGEA article](https://lpvageojs02.it.purdue.edu/ojs/index.php/jgea/article/view/338); it depends on the closed-source PATH solver, which without a licence is limited to 300 variables and 2,000 non-zeros — [MPSGE.jl repository mirror](https://gh.r-mj.com/julia-mpsge/MPSGE.jl). StandardCGE.jl is an MIT-licensed Julia implementation of the Hosoe–Gasawa–Hashimoto textbook CGE — [StandardCGE.jl mirror](https://gh.r-mj.com/equicirco/StandardCGE.jl)
- Dynamic non-equilibrium IO works empirically: Pichler, Pangallo, del Rio-Chanona, Lafond & Farmer (JEDC 2022) built a dynamic disequilibrium input-output model with industry-specific production functions (from a survey of industry analysts) and made accurate real-time forecasts of UK sectoral and aggregate output for Q2 2020 — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9472492), [INET WP 2021-18](https://www.inet.ox.ac.uk/publications/no-2021-18-in-and-out-of-lockdown-propagation-of-supply-and-demand-shocks-in-a-dynamic-input-output-model)
- That model has been ported and re-validated for Belgium, with household demand shocks calibrated to 115 time series, and a sensitivity analysis over eight parameters — [Alleman et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/a-dynamic-disequilibrium-input-output-model-of-the-belgian-xfum6ghvcv25), [earlier Belgian validation](https://scispace.com/papers/validating-a-dynamic-input-output-model-for-the-propagation-2xey34si)
- An ABM framework can read a SAM, supply-use table or IO table and "self-deploy" an economy that reproduces it — [Jaraíz 2022](https://scispace.com/papers/an-agent-based-modeling-approach-for-real-world-economic-3i1lmw02)

### Inferences
- Researcher micro-benchmark: a direct Leontief solve costs about 70 µs at 64 sectors, 0.5 ms at 163 and 5 ms at 500; a single matrix-vector step of a partial-adjustment dynamic IO model costs about 2 µs at 64 sectors. Sector count is not a performance constraint at any resolution the open data supports.
- Practical simplified forms for a game, in rising order of cost and realism: (a) static Leontief quantity model for demand-driven output and employment by sector; (b) Leontief price model for cost-push pass-through of taxes, wages, energy and import prices; (c) dynamic IO with inventories and partial adjustment (Pichler et al. style), which also handles supply shocks and bottlenecks; (d) a "CGE-lite" with a few substitution elasticities (import versus domestic, energy versus other) evaluated explicitly from last period's prices, without a market-clearing solve.
- Full CGE is the wrong tool: it presumes market clearing each period (no unemployment or inflation dynamics unless added), the canonical database is paid and not redistributable, the standard open formulation leans on a proprietary solver, and nonlinear complementarity solves can fail after extreme player inputs.
- 45–64 industries is the natural ceiling because that is what FIGARO/OECD publish; for player legibility the UI can aggregate to 10–20 sectors while the engine runs at full resolution.

### Gaps
- Sector count and time step of the Pichler et al. model not confirmed in-session (my recollection: 55 WIOD industries, daily step).
- No verified equation counts or solve times for GTAP, ORANI or MAGNET; no licence statement found specific to the OECD ICIO tables (see section 9 for OECD's general CC BY 4.0 policy).
- ORANI-G, MAGNET, GTAPinGAMS and any open Python CGE package: not found by search this session.

## 4. DSGE and semi-structural central-bank / finance-ministry models

### Takeaway
Institutional workhorse models are semi-structural systems of a few hundred equations (mostly identities plus 40–60 estimated behavioural equations, typically error-correction form); FRB/US and the OBR model publish their full equation listings. They are the best source of equation specifications and sanity-check responses to borrow, while microfounded DSGE is a poor fit for a game (rational-expectations solution methods, weak accounting detail, hard to explain).

### Cited Findings
- FRB/US is a large-scale quarterly model of the US economy in which most behavioural equations derive from optimising behaviour with explicit expectations of firms, households and financial markets — [Brayton & Tinsley 1996](https://scispace.com/papers/a-guide-to-frb-us-a-macroeconomic-model-of-the-united-states-1vegeu6fvl); it is one of several models Board staff use for forecasting and for monetary and fiscal analysis — [Brayton, Laubach & Reifschneider 2014](https://scispace.com/papers/the-frb-us-model-a-tool-for-macroeconomic-policy-analysis-3hfnzqw98m)
- FRB/US size: 284 equations and 365 variables in the February 2024 version — [R Consortium post](https://r-consortium.org/posts/us-federal-reserve-quarterly-model-in-r/); another source counts roughly 375 equations of which about 50 are "core" stochastic equations — [arXiv 2008.08705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.08705). The difference likely reflects versions and counting conventions.
- The Federal Reserve distributes `pyfrbus`, a Python platform for running FRB/US simulations; the package contains the model equations (stored in an XML file), simulation code, example programs and documentation, with the database distributed separately; source is distributed for EViews and Python only — [Federal Reserve, FRB/US in Python](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-python.htm). An R port exists via the `bimets` package — [bimets FRB/US vignette](https://mirror.las.iastate.edu/CRAN/web/packages/bimets/vignettes/frb2bimets.pdf)
- OBR macroeconomic model: documented in Briefing Paper No. 5, which includes the Winsolve model code as an annex — [OBR Briefing Paper 5](https://obr.uk/download/briefing-paper-no-5-the-macroeconomic-model); updated model variables and code files were posted on 15 October 2025 — [OBR model page](https://obr.uk/forecasts-in-depth/obr-macroeconomic-model/). Relationships are grouped into accounting identities (National Accounts definitions), behavioural equations (usually error-correction form) and technical relationships.
- The predecessor HM Treasury model (March 2008 documentation) had around 40 estimated equations plus about 320 technical relationships and identities, 518 variables in total — [HM Treasury model documentation, deposited paper](https://data.parliament.uk/DepositedPapers/Files/DEP2009-2901/DEP2009-2901.pdf)
- The OBR also maintains a "small economic model" and publishes ready reckoners — [OBR small model update](https://articles.obr.uk/an-update-to-the-obrs-small-economic-model/index.html), [OBR ready reckoner](https://articles.obr.uk/the-obr-ready-reckoner). The April 2025 OBR–HMT memorandum describes the main model as "a mixture of behavioural equations and identities to ensure consistency across the OBR economic forecast" — [OBR–HMT MoU](https://obr.uk/docs/dlm_uploads/MOU_between_OBR_and_HMT_for_macroeconomic_models_April_2025.pdf)
- ECB-BASE (ECB WP 2315, 2019) is a large-scale estimated semi-structural model for the euro area that converges to a well-defined steady state and serves as a workhorse for forecasting and policy simulation; its successor ECB-MC covers Germany, France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ecb/ecbwps/20192315.html), [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/introducing-ecb-base-the-blueprint-of-the-new-ecb-semi-w7pnq1ttxj). It has been used to compare fiscal and monetary rules to 2030 — [Bańkowski et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/assessing-the-fiscal-monetary-policy-mix-in-the-euro-area-qs7b2h3xgeh5)
- The Bank of Canada relies mainly on ToTEM (DSGE) and LENS (large empirical semi-structural), and states that adding detailed household and firm heterogeneity and realistic behaviour to them is difficult "both in theory and in practice" — [BoC SWP 2022-51](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2022/12/staff-working-paper-2022-51/)
- The World Bank's MFMod macro-structural model is run for 115 countries, with 1,000 stochastic simulations per country in one exercise — [World Bank working paper](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099516206222334188/pdf/IDU0847415ac0dc78040150bdd90714b239a7bb9.pdf); it can be paired with a microsimulation module for distributional analysis — [World Bank macro models overview](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/490571642086593026/pdf/World-Bank-Group-Macroeconomic-Models-for-Climate-Policy-Analysis.pdf)

### Inferences
- What is realistic to borrow: (1) the three-way split of equations (identities / estimated behavioural / technical), which is also how an SFC engine is organised; (2) error-correction specifications, which give each variable a long-run target plus a speed of adjustment, a form that is easy to parameterise, explain and expose as a slider; (3) published simulation properties (multipliers, ready reckoners) as validation targets for the game model; (4) FRB/US's option of backward-looking (VAR-based) expectations, which avoids solving for model-consistent expectations.
- What not to borrow: forward-looking rational expectations (requires solving the whole future path each turn and breaks when the player behaves "unexpectedly"), and the models' US/UK-specific estimated coefficients as if they were universal.
- A semi-structural model of 300–500 equations is the same computational size class as an empirical SFC model, so the per-turn cost conclusion from sections 1–2 applies.

### Gaps
- Licence of `pyfrbus` not confirmed (US federal works are generally public domain, but check the package).
- No in-session sources for NiGEM, the European Commission's QUEST, OECD models, or Dynare/DSGE.jl/OG-Core as open tools; ECB-BASE equation count not found.
- My recollection (verify) is that FRB/US documentation describes about 60 stochastic equations, 320 identities and 125 exogenous variables.

## 5. Macroeconomic agent-based models

### Takeaway
Data-driven macro ABMs now match VAR/DSGE forecast accuracy and can be initialised from national accounts, IO tables and census data, but 1:1-scale versions involve millions of agents and HPC, and calibration needs simulation budgets a small team cannot casually afford. A notable finding for a game is that forecasting performance appears insensitive to scale, so a 1:1000 model (thousands of agents) is the relevant size class if agents are used at all.

### Cited Findings
- Poledna, Miess, Hommes & Rabitsch, "Economic forecasting with an agent-based model" (European Economic Review 151, 2023, art. 104306): described as the first ABM to compete with benchmark VAR and DSGE models in out-of-sample macro forecasting; a small-open-economy model for Austria using micro and macro data from national and sector accounts, input-output tables, government statistics, census and business demography; covers all ESA 2010 activities with "millions of heterogeneous agents" and gives sector-level forecasts — [IIASA record](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/18339), [SciSpace abstract](https://scispace.com/papers/economic-forecasting-with-an-agent-based-model-410qdiiwpw)
- BeforeIT.jl (Glielmo, Devetak, Meligrana, Poledna; arXiv Feb 2025; Banca d'Italia): open-source Julia implementation of that model family, AGPL-3.0 — [arXiv 2502.13267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13267v1), [GitHub](https://github.com/bancaditalia/BeforeIT.jl). Its benchmark measures mean time per model step for the Austrian calibration at about 8,000 agents (scale 1:1000) and about 8 million agents (1:1): arXiv v1 reports BeforeIT about 17x faster than the original Matlab and 4x faster than Matlab-generated C single-threaded at the small scale, and 35x and 10x at 8 million agents; the later HTML version reports about 20x/4x and 43x/12x — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13267)
- HPC implementation of the same model class: 331 million agents simulated "within 108 seconds using 128 CPU cores" with MPI + OpenMP hybrid parallelism and about 70% strong scalability, aimed at 1:1 simulation of economies the size of the eurozone — [Gill et al., IEEE TPDS 2021](https://scispace.com/papers/high-performance-computing-implementations-of-agent-based-2tqjy37lns). A related HPC extension generated 127 million agents for Japan by disaggregating macro data — [Gill et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/seamless-simulations-of-earthquake-disasters-and-economy-at-qblir1mtlmri)
- Scale may not matter for accuracy: an Italian replication reports that "the scale is not relevant for the forecasting performances", and that forecasts are often "not very far from" basic econometric models — [Di Domenico, Catalano & Riccetti](https://scispace.com/papers/a-large-scale-data-driven-agent-based-model-for-the-italian-2jpoaqwrks)
- CANVAS (Bank of Canada SWP 2022-51; Hommes, He, Poledna, Siqueira, Zhang): Canadian behavioural ABM with household and firm heterogeneity, adaptive learning and heuristics, and the Canadian production network; forecasts compared against a VAR and the ToTEM DSGE — [Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2022/12/staff-working-paper-2022-51/)
- INET Oxford (Wiese, Kaszowska-Mojsa, Dyer, Moran, Pangallo, Lafond, Muellbauer, Calinescu, Farmer; WP 2024-06): extends the Poledna model with housing markets and synthetic populations with income, wealth and consumption heterogeneity; calibrated for all 38 OECD countries with approximate Bayesian inference; reports outperforming the Poledna model and an AR(1) — [INET Oxford](https://www.inet.ox.ac.uk/publications/no-2024-06-forecasting-macroeconomic-dynamics-using-a-calibrated-data-driven-agent-based-model), [arXiv 2409.18760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18760v1)
- Axtell, "120 Million Agents Self-Organize into 6 Million Firms" (AAMAS 2016): full-scale US private sector, calibrated to firm sizes, ages, growth rates, job tenure and labour flows; a coalition-formation model with unstable Nash equilibria; "made possible by large-scale, parallel agent computing" — [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/120-million-agents-self-organize-into-6-million-firms-a-4tn093nlgr), [AAMAS PDF](https://ifmas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2016/pdfs/p806.pdf)
- Other 1:1 examples: a Hungarian housing-market ABM with all 4 million households — [Mérő et al., JEDC 2023](https://scispace.com/papers/a-high-resolution-data-driven-agent-based-model-of-the-4a414n6ift)
- EURACE / Eurace@Unibi are implemented in the FLAME framework — [Bielefeld ETACE](https://www.uni-bielefeld.de/fakultaeten/wirtschaftswissenschaften/lehrbereiche/etace/eurace@unibi/flame-simulation-framewor/); a EURACE performance deliverable indicates runs on the order of 30,000 agents, short of the project's goal of hundreds of thousands (search-summary level; exact deliverable not confirmed) — [EURACE D8.4](https://uni-bielefeld.de/fakultaeten/wirtschaftswissenschaften/lehrbereiche/etace/eurace@unibi/eurace-deliverables/EURACE.Deliverable.D8.4-(1).pdf)
- "Keynes meeting Schumpeter" (K+S; Dosi, Fagiolo, Roventini, JEDC 2010 and successors): capital-good firms doing R&D and consumption-good firms buying machines, later with banks, a central bank and government; analysed by Monte Carlo simulation — [LEM WP 2008/21](https://www.lem.sssup.it/WPLem/files/2008-21.pdf), [K+S family overview, 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/micro-and-macro-policies-in-the-keynes-schumpeter-1047cdzhax)
- Mark-0 (Gualdi, Tarzia, Zamponi, Bouchaud, JEDC 2015): a deliberately minimal ABM characterised by a "phase diagram"; generic phase transition between low- and high-unemployment regimes; pseudo-code in the appendix; applied to COVID recovery shapes and post-COVID inflation — [arXiv 1307.5319](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.5319), [Sharma et al. 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/v-u-l-or-w-shaped-economic-recovery-after-covid-19-insights-1dgtz0o83n), [Knicker et al. 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/post-covid-inflation-and-the-monetary-policy-dilemma-an-5f5mdm0b8u)
- AB-SFC benchmark model and the JMAB Java platform (Caiani, Godin, Caverzasi, Gallegati, Kinsella, Stiglitz, JEDC 2016): agent-based with full stock-flow consistent accounting — [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/agent-based-stock-flow-consistent-macroeconomics-towards-a-t5b49rvsef), [JMAB](https://scispace.com/papers/innovation-demand-and-finance-in-an-agent-based-stock-flow-1mo2gowdqx)
- Calibration difficulty: ABMs "lack a tractable likelihood function"; some likelihood-free methods "typically require many hundreds of thousands of simulations" — [Dyer, Cannon & Farmer, JEDC 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/black-box-bayesian-inference-for-agent-based-models-56jzu496i0); for large-scale ABMs, methods needing in-the-loop simulation such as the method of simulated moments "may not be feasible" — [Barde & van der Hoog 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/an-empirical-validation-protocol-for-large-scale-agent-based-3uqiy7f1km)
- A classification and review of data-driven economic ABMs (initialisation from micro-data, tracking empirical time series, calibration and data assimilation) — [Pangallo & del Rio-Chanona 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/data-driven-economic-agent-based-models-wv552j70w6cz)

### Inferences
- Macro ABMs split into two size classes: stylised models (Mark-0, K+S, CATS, AB-SFC benchmark) with hundreds to thousands of agents that run on a laptop, and data-driven 1:1 models (Poledna line, Axtell) with 10^6–10^8 agents that need compiled, parallel code. Only the first class, or the second at 1:1000 scale, is compatible with a consumer PC and thousands of headless runs.
- ABMs are stochastic. A game then needs either fixed seeds, averaging over replications (multiplying cost), or acceptance that identical policies give different outcomes. That is defensible as "uncertainty" but makes balancing and bug reproduction harder.
- Emergent phase transitions (Mark-0's good/bad economy tipping points) are attractive gameplay but hostile to tuning: small parameter changes flip regimes, and a player cannot easily be told why the economy collapsed.
- AGPL-3.0 on BeforeIT.jl makes it unsuitable to embed in a closed-source commercial game; it remains valuable as a reference for data pipelines (which Eurostat tables map to which initial conditions).
- A middle path consistent with the evidence is to use agents only where heterogeneity changes answers (household tax/benefit incidence; maybe a few hundred representative firms per sector) and keep everything else aggregate.

- Absolute per-step runtimes for BeforeIT.jl, agent breakdown and industry count for the Poledna model, the number of Monte Carlo replications, and the simulated horizon behind the "108 seconds" HPC figure were not retrievable (arxiv.org and iiasa.ac.at blocked). My recollection (verify): 64 industries, quarterly step, Matlab original, 500 Monte Carlo runs per forecast.

## 6. Tax-benefit microsimulation

### Takeaway
Static tax-benefit microsimulation applies coded policy rules to a weighted household sample and is fast (vectorised array operations over 10^4–10^6 records), deterministic and directly explainable ("this policy costs X and hits decile Y"). The rule engines are open (OpenFisca and PolicyEngine under AGPL, EUROMOD under EUPL with CC BY rules), but the representative microdata are the binding constraint.

### Cited Findings
- EUROMOD is the EU tax-benefit microsimulation model covering policy scope, input data, validation, a tax-benefit programming language and a user interface — [Sutherland & Figari 2013](https://scispace.com/papers/euromod-the-european-union-tax-benefit-microsimulation-model-1rjrku56q1). Its source code is open under EUPL-1.2 on GitHub; the coded policy rules are CC BY 4.0 since release I3.0+; the compiled installer has its own end-user licence — [JRC EUROMOD download](https://euromod-web.jrc.ec.europa.eu/download-euromod), [JRC model inventory](https://web.jrc.ec.europa.eu/policy-model-inventory/explore/models/model-euromod). A Python connector is on PyPI — [euromod on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/euromod/0.2.5)
- EUROMOD input data for the 27 member states require a Research Project Proposal approved by Eurostat (EU-SILC and now Household Budget Survey data) — [JRC EUROMOD access](https://euromod-web.jrc.ec.europa.eu/download-euromod)
- UKMOD is the UK counterpart distributed by the Centre for Microsimulation and Policy Analysis — [UKMOD access](https://www.microsimulation.ac.uk/ukmod/access/)
- OpenFisca: free software under the GNU AGPL, written in Python, started by the French government in 2011; the engine is country-independent and about ten countries have modelled their systems — [Interoperable Europe](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/justice-law-and-security/solution/openfisca), [code.gouv.fr ecosystem](https://ecosystem.code.gouv.fr/hosts/GitHub/repositories/openfisca%2Fopenfisca-core). A critical account notes it "encoded the rules; it didn't solve the data challenge" — [Society in Silico, ch. 3](https://societyinsilico.pages.dev/book/open-source-revolution)
- PolicyEngine (an OpenFisca-derived stack): `policyengine-uk` and `policyengine.py` are AGPL-3.0 — [policyengine-uk](https://www.github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk), [PolicyEngine on GitHub](https://github.com/PolicyEngine). The UK model uses an "enhanced FRS" (Family Resources Survey with imputations and reweighting to official statistics); HM Treasury has published an algorithmic transparency record for its use of PolicyEngine UK alongside its in-house IGOTM model — [gov.uk transparency record](https://gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/hmt-modelling-policy-engine). The US model uses an enhanced CPS with PUF-based income imputations and SCF wealth, distributed as HDF5 — [policyengine-us-data docs](https://policyengine.github.io/policyengine-us-data/introduction)
- Performance indications: a Rust reimplementation, `policyengine-uk-rust`, is described as about 0.1 ms per household — [policyengine-uk-rust](https://policyengine.github.io/policyengine-uk-rust); a PolicyEngine TANF calculator measured one vectorised pass at 4.3 s versus about 47.6 minutes cell-by-cell (about 664x) — [PolicyEngine tanf-calculator PR 10 (via mirror)](https://github.com/PolicyEngine/tanf-calculator/pull/10)
- PSL Tax-Calculator (US federal income and payroll tax): ships a CPS-based public input file built by `taxdata`; the IRS-SOI Public Use File variant is proprietary and not distributed — [Tax-Calculator data docs](https://PSLmodels.github.io/Tax-Calculator/usage/data.html), [taxdata](https://github.com/PSLmodels/taxdata). A companion module estimates partial-equilibrium behavioural responses — [Behavioral-Responses](https://github.com/PSLmodels/Behavioral-Responses)
- OECD TaxBEN: a calculator for hypothetical ("model") families across countries, not a population microsimulation; written in Stata; code is not open (listed as non-free, not accessible on request), though a public web calculator and the methodology manual exist — [JRC inventory, TaxBEN](https://web.jrc.ec.europa.eu/policy-model-inventory/explore/models/model-taxben/quality-transparency), [OECD TaxBEN manual](https://oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/income-support-redistribution-and-work-incentives/oecd-taxben-methodology-and-manual.pdf)

### Inferences
- Researcher micro-benchmark: a four-bracket income tax plus a tapered benefit over a synthetic household array took about 0.1 ms for 10,000 households, 1.2 ms for 100,000 and 25 ms for 1,000,000 per full pass in NumPy. A realistic system with dozens of instruments would be perhaps 10–100 times that, still well inside a turn at 10^4–10^5 households.
- A sample of 10,000–50,000 weighted synthetic households is enough to produce decile tables, poverty and inequality indices and budget costs, and it lets the game ask "who wins and who loses", which drives the voter model that a Democracy-style game needs anyway.
- AGPL engines cannot be linked into a closed-source game. EUROMOD's CC BY 4.0 policy-rule files are the most reusable asset as a parameter and design reference for 27 countries; OpenFisca/PolicyEngine are best used as design inspiration (entity model: person / benefit unit / household; variables as vectorised formulas over periods; parameters as dated values).
- The household microdata must be synthetic: build a population from published marginals (income distribution by decile, household composition, employment status, tenure, region) and reweight to match national-accounts totals. This avoids every microdata licence problem and keeps the shipped file small.

## 7. Hybrid designs in practice, and the cleanest hybrid for a game

### Takeaway
Institutions almost always run a macro model and a microsimulation as separate layers linked top-down (macro aggregates rescale the micro sample), occasionally with iteration back up; fully integrated models are rare and lose data structure. For a game the cleanest hybrid is an SFC accounting skeleton at institutional-sector level, a dynamic IO production block, and a synthetic-household microsimulation for taxes, benefits and consumption, linked top-down with one lagged bottom-up feedback.

### Cited Findings
- The standard taxonomy of macro–micro linkage is: representative household, fully integrated, top-down, bottom-up and iterative; for the iterative technique "convergence is not guaranteed and must be verified for each simulation" — [Cockburn, Savard & Tiberti 2014, "Macro-Micro Models"](https://ideas.repec.org/h/eme/ceazzz/s0573-855520140000293008.html)
- Building the same reform three ways (fully integrated, top-down, top-down/bottom-up) on the same economy gives different results, especially for income distribution and poverty — [Colombo, IJM 2010](https://scispace.com/papers/linking-cge-and-microsimulation-models-a-comparison-of-2zwafynf2k)
- Top-down linkage, where CGE aggregates feed the microsimulation, is the most commonly used approach — [Feltenstein et al. 2013](https://scispace.com/papers/the-impact-of-micro-simulation-and-cge-modeling-on-tax-f4hrettqwx); within top-down, simple reweighting of the sample "can constitute a good alternative" to a behavioural micro model when data or time are limited — [Hérault, IJM 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/sequential-linking-of-computable-general-equilibrium-and-3zkjdxvyhd)
- Different combinations (layered or integrated, or a single model type) suit different problems — [Davies, IJM 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/combining-microsimulation-with-cge-and-macro-modelling-for-45sdw92c0w)
- A recent central-bank example: Latvijas Banka links a CGE model with EUROMOD by an iterative top-down/bottom-up loop that converges on disposable income, employment and wages — [Beņkovskis et al., Latvijas Banka WP 1/2023](https://datnes.latvijasbanka.lv/papers/WP_1-2023.pdf)
- World Bank GIDD is a top-down framework that distributes macro shocks across household surveys; the macro model leads and micro-to-macro feedback is treated as second-order — [Bussolo et al., IJM](https://microsimulation.pub/articles/00027); MFMod can be paired with it — [World Bank macro models overview](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/490571642086593026/pdf/World-Bank-Group-Macroeconomic-Models-for-Climate-Policy-Analysis.pdf)
- UK practice: HM Treasury costs tax measures with a microsimulation model of the tax system and DWP's Policy Simulation Model, which take the OBR's economic determinants as inputs; costings are static with behavioural adjustments layered on, and the OBR certifies them — [HM Treasury, Budget 2016 policy costings](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/508147/PU1912_Policy_Costings_FINAL3.pdf). The OBR's labour-supply module runs on HMT's IGOTM microsimulation platform — [OBR–HMT MoU, April 2025](https://obr.uk/docs/dlm_uploads/MOU_between_OBR_and_HMT_for_macroeconomic_models_April_2025.pdf)
- Precedents for the proposed combination exist piecewise: SFC + IO (Berg et al. 2015), SFC + agents (Caiani et al. 2016), SAM-initialised agents (Jaraíz 2022), and FIGARO-NAM as an official multi-country SAM-like dataset — [Berg et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/a-stock-flow-consistent-input-output-model-with-applications-1mva54buee), [Caiani et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/agent-based-stock-flow-consistent-macroeconomics-towards-a-t5b49rvsef), [Jaraíz](https://scispace.com/papers/an-agent-based-modeling-approach-for-real-world-economic-3i1lmw02), [FIGARO-NAM 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-statistical-working-papers/w/ks-01-25-050)

### Inferences
- Comparison of paradigms against the game constraint (my synthesis of sections 1–6; costs are order-of-magnitude judgements, not literature values):

| Paradigm | Typical state size | Per-turn cost on a PC | Initialising data | Calibration burden | Explainable to a player | Fit as game core |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Democracy-style normalised graph | 10^2–10^3 nodes | microseconds | none (hand-authored) | hand tuning | high | baseline; no real units |
| System dynamics | 10^1–10^3 equations | microseconds to ms | aggregates | judgement plus history fit | high | good, lacks enforced accounting |
| SFC | 10^1–10^3 equations, 5–8 sectors | microseconds to ms | national and sector accounts, flow of funds | moderate (behavioural equations) | high (matrices) | strong core |
| IO / dynamic IO | 45–64 industries | microseconds to ms | FIGARO / OECD ICIO / WIOD | low (coefficients from tables) | medium | strong add-on |
| Full CGE | 10^3–10^5 equations | seconds; solver can fail | SAM plus elasticities (GTAP paid) | high | low | poor |
| Semi-structural (FRB/US, OBR) | 300–500 equations | ms | long time series | high (econometrics) | medium | borrow equations |
| DSGE | 10^1–10^2 equations | needs expectations solution | time series | high | low | poor |
| Stylised ABM | 10^2–10^4 agents | ms to seconds, stochastic | stylised facts | high, unstable regimes | low to medium | optional flavour |
| Data-driven 1:1 ABM | 10^6–10^8 agents | HPC | accounts, IO, census, registers | very high | low | not feasible |
| Tax-benefit microsimulation | 10^4–10^6 households | ms | synthetic or licensed survey | low (rules are law) | very high | strong add-on |

- Recommended hybrid: (1) SFC core with households, non-financial firms, banks, central bank, government and rest of world, holding all balance sheets and enforcing identities; (2) dynamic IO block at FIGARO/ICIO resolution that turns final demand into sectoral output, employment, imports and cost-push prices; (3) a synthetic household sample that computes taxes, benefits and consumption by household, whose weighted totals are the household row of the transaction matrix; (4) a small set of semi-structural behavioural equations in error-correction form (wages/prices, investment, interest rates, exchange rate) with literature-ranged parameters.
- Linking rule: top-down each turn (macro wages, employment and prices rescale and re-employ the household sample), with the micro totals (tax revenue, benefit spend, consumption) feeding the macro block for the same or the next turn. Using a one-turn lag for the bottom-up feedback removes the convergence problem Cockburn et al. warn about, at the price of a quarter's delay.
- Because every layer is deterministic and array-based, a full turn should cost low milliseconds in compiled code, leaving thousands of headless runs per minute for calibration and balancing.
- Colombo's result (different linkages give different distributional answers) is a warning that the linkage is itself a modelling assumption and should be documented and tested, not treated as plumbing.

### Gaps
- Peichl (2009), "The benefits and problems of linking micro and macro models", and Bourguignon, Robilliard & Robinson (2003) were not retrieved directly; they are the usual citations for this taxonomy.
- No in-session sources for IMF toolkits (e.g. DIGNAD), IFS TAXBEN, or the detail of how the OBR maps the macro forecast into the fiscal forecast.
- I found no published example of exactly the SFC + IO + household-microsimulation triple in a single open model; COMPASS (section 2) and the INET Oxford model (section 5) are the nearest and should be examined.

## 8. Behavioural responses the game must parameterise

### Takeaway
For almost every key behavioural parameter the literature gives a range wide enough to change policy conclusions (spending multipliers roughly 0.6–1 on average and higher in slumps, ETI estimates from under 0.1 to over 0.8, Frisch elasticities from near 0 to above 1). That strongly supports exposing contested parameters as named, sourced presets or sliders with a defensible default.

### Cited Findings
- Fiscal multipliers, meta-analysis: Gechert (Oxford Economic Papers 2015) pools 104 studies and 1,069 multipliers; public spending multipliers are close to one, about 0.3–0.4 above tax and transfer multipliers; public investment multipliers exceed general spending by about 0.5; results vary with study design — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:oxecpp:v:67:y:2015:i:3:p:553-580.). Multipliers are significantly higher in downturns — [Gechert & Rannenberg, VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/fiscal-multipliers-downturns-and-effects-eurozone-consolidation)
- Ramey (JEP 2019): "the bulk of the estimates for average spending and tax change multipliers lie in a fairly narrow range, 0.6 to 1 for spending multipliers and -2 to -3 for tax change multipliers", with identified circumstances outside those ranges — [NBER w25531](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25531)
- Note the conflict: Gechert finds spending multipliers above tax multipliers, while Ramey reports narrative-identified tax multipliers of −2 to −3, larger in absolute size than spending multipliers — [Gechert](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:oxecpp:v:67:y:2015:i:3:p:553-580.); contradicted by [Ramey](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25531)
- IMF guidance (Batini, Eyraud, Forni & Weber 2014): where no estimate exists, a "bucket approach" assigns a country to a multiplier group from structural characteristics (e.g. low trade openness defined as imports below 30% of domestic demand, exchange-rate regime) and adjusts for the cycle; output effects of permanent measures typically vanish within five years — [IMF technical note](https://scispace.com/papers/fiscal-multipliers-size-determinants-and-use-in-3dbgnfvc7u), [bucket approach WP](https://scispace.com/papers/a-simple-method-to-compute-fiscal-multipliers-49pvywla7k). An application to Egypt yields 0.3–0.6 — [Youssef et al. 2025](https://scispace.com/papers/qys-hjm-md-f-lnfq-lhkwmy-fy-msr-bstkhdm-nhj-ldlw-bucket-2roqbhc1b7ma)
- Elasticity of taxable income: the critical review is Saez, Slemrod & Giertz (JEL 2012) — [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/the-elasticity-of-taxable-income-with-respect-to-marginal-l1jb9rc9oy); a midpoint of about 0.25 is attributed to them (secondary source) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxable_income_elasticity). Neisser's meta-regression (Economic Journal 2021; 51 studies, 1,420 estimates) finds heterogeneity driven by method, sample and country, and that the ETI is endogenous to the tax system — [SciSpace](https://scispace.com/papers/the-elasticity-of-taxable-income-a-meta-regression-analysis-1zz0098b42); the working-paper version reports average before-deduction elasticities of 0.053–0.120, stylised after-deduction estimates of 0.074–0.827, implied revenue-maximising top rates of 44.63%–90.01%, and selective reporting bias — [Neisser WP](https://epflicht.ulb.uni-bonn.de/download/pdf/698662)
- Laffer curves: Trabandt & Uhlig find the US could raise revenue 30% via labour taxes and 6% via capital taxes, the EU-14 only 8% and 1%; in the EU-14, 54% of a labour tax cut and 79% of a capital tax cut are self-financing; the consumption-tax Laffer curve has no peak — [Trabandt & Uhlig](https://scispace.com/papers/how-far-are-we-from-the-slippery-slope-the-laffer-curve-55yk915k3j)
- Labour supply: Chetty, Guren, Manoli & Weber (2011) recommend Hicksian elasticities of 0.3 (intensive) and 0.25 (extensive) and Frisch elasticities of 0.5 (intensive) and 0.25 (extensive) — [NBER w16729](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16729.pdf). A CBO review (Reichling & Whalen 2012) notes micro-based Frisch estimates on the intensive margin range "from zero to more than 1.0", and a 2024 meta-analysis of over 700 estimates finds a raw mean near 0.5 that falls to about 0.2 on the intensive margin (and very small on the extensive margin) after correcting for publication bias (both from search summaries; URLs not pinned to a single document) — [search-result source](https://unipi.gr/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/frisch.pdf)
- Okun's law: a strong and fairly stable relationship in most countries, but the coefficient "varies substantially across countries", partly reflecting national labour-market features — [Ball, Leigh & Loungani, JMCB 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/okun-s-law-fit-at-50-1b7z9mwtfa)
- Phillips curve: estimated across US states the slope "is small and was small even during the early 1980s", with only a modest decline since; the 1980s disinflation and later stability are attributed mostly to long-run inflation expectations — [Hazell, Herreño, Nakamura & Steinsson, QJE 2022](https://scispace.com/papers/oup-accepted-manuscript-3uxz4xdo)
- Exchange-rate pass-through: partial in the short run across 25 OECD countries, with producer-currency pricing more prevalent in the long run — [Campa & Goldberg](https://scispace.com/papers/exchange-rate-pass-through-into-import-prices-4tdlrsoamx); US import-price pass-through fell from above 0.5 in the 1980s to around 0.2 — [Marazzi, Sheets & Vigfusson 2005](https://scispace.com/papers/exchange-rate-pass-through-to-u-s-import-prices-some-new-332izu9a9v); emerging markets with single-digit inflation show pass-through close to advanced-economy levels — [Ca' Zorzi, Hahn & Sánchez 2007](https://scispace.com/papers/exchange-rate-pass-through-in-emerging-markets-1-4mefqv1wrk); determinants include income, tariffs, country size, wages, long-run inflation and exchange-rate variability — [Frankel, Parsley & Wei 2012](https://scispace.com/papers/slow-passthrough-around-the-world-a-new-import-for-1e5ku5g81d)
- Debt dynamics: safe interest rates below growth rates are "more the historical norm than the exception" in the US, so debt rollovers may be feasible, though with welfare costs via lower capital accumulation and multiple-equilibrium risk — [Blanchard, AER 2019](https://scispace.com/papers/public-debt-and-low-interest-rates-wxbgtewoyr); r − g is often negative in advanced economies but highly variable over the medium term — [Mehrotra & Sergeyev 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/debt-sustainability-in-a-low-interest-rate-world-346eq5bfj4)

### Inferences
- Disagreement is structural, not noise: multipliers depend on the state of the cycle, monetary reaction, openness and exchange-rate regime; the ETI depends on the tax base and avoidance opportunities (Neisser: "endogenous to the tax system"); the Okun coefficient depends on labour-market institutions. The game can model these dependencies explicitly (multiplier emerges from import leakage, saving rates and the central-bank rule in the SFC/IO core) so that only the deep parameters are free.
- Recommended treatment: ship a "mainstream consensus" default set with each parameter annotated by source and range, plus named presets (e.g. "supply-side optimist", "Keynesian", "institutional forecaster") and an expert screen for direct edits. Because many multipliers will be emergent, the calibration target is that the default set reproduces published ranges (e.g. Ramey's 0.6–1) in headless impulse-response tests.
- The Phillips-curve evidence argues for a flat short-run slope plus an explicit, de-anchorable expectations term (as in the Mark-0 inflation extension in section 5); this also gives the player a meaningful central-bank credibility mechanic.
- The debt block needs no elasticity: with SFC accounting, debt/GDP dynamics follow from the identity d' = d(1+r)/(1+g) − primary balance; the contested part is how the interest rate responds to debt, which should be a preset-governed risk-premium function.

### Gaps
- Numeric coefficients not retrieved in-session. My recollections (verify before use): Okun coefficient about −0.4 to −0.5 for the US with a range from roughly −0.15 (Japan) to −0.85 (Spain) in Ball et al.; Hazell et al. slope about 0.006; Saez–Slemrod–Giertz long-run ETI range 0.12–0.40; OBR June 2010 impact multipliers of 0.35 (VAT), 0.3 (income tax/NICs), 0.6 (welfare and current spending) and 1.0 (capital spending).
- OBR ready-reckoner values and CBO's published multiplier ranges were not retrieved; the OBR page is [here](https://articles.obr.uk/the-obr-ready-reckoner).
- Consumer-price (as opposed to import-price) pass-through estimates and Burstein & Gopinath's handbook survey were not retrieved.
- No source retrieved on consumption responses (marginal propensities to consume by income group), investment elasticities or trade elasticities, all of which the game also needs.

## 9. Public data sources and redistribution rights

### Takeaway
Aggregate macro, sector-account and input-output data from OECD (CC BY 4.0 since July 2024), WIOD (CC BY 4.0) and, with care, Eurostat are usable in a commercial product with attribution; the restricted items are household microdata (EU-SILC, HBS, HFCS, national surveys), GTAP, and share-alike EXIOBASE. The safe pattern is to ship derived, calibrated parameters and a synthetic population, not raw third-party tables or microdata.

### Cited Findings
- OECD: content published from 1 July 2024 is under CC BY 4.0 by default (commercial use permitted with attribution); earlier content falls under older terms, and specific datasets may carry third-party restrictions — [OECD press release, July 2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2024/07/oecd-data-publications-and-analysis-become-freely-accessible.html), [OECD open-by-default policy](https://oecd.org/en/about/oecd-open-by-default-policy.html), [OECD terms](https://oecd.org/termsandconditions/)
- WIOD: CC BY 4.0 — [GGDC](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/blog/blog-08-11-2016-release-of-new-wiod-data). EXIOBASE 3: CC BY-SA 4.0 with a planned non-commercial/dual licence from 3.9 — [exiobase site](https://exiobase.iedl.no). GTAP: paid licence — [GTAP pricing](https://gtap.agecon.purdue.edu/databases/pricing.asp)
- EU-SILC scientific-use files are released to researchers under an approved research proposal; secure-use files only in Eurostat's safe centre — [Eurostat/CESSDA presentation](https://dmeg.cessda.eu/content/download/4375/49208/file/microdata%20at%20eurostat%20-%20eusilc.pdf). EUROMOD input data likewise need a Eurostat-approved Research Project Proposal — [JRC](https://euromod-web.jrc.ec.europa.eu/download-euromod)
- EU-SILC public-use files are fully synthetic, downloadable without registration, intended for training and testing, with "limited research value"; initially seven countries (AT, DE, FI, FR, HU, NL, SI) for 2012–2013, with more countries and years added later; they carry a disclaimer that results are at the user's sole risk — [Eurostat CROS, PUF EU-SILC](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/puf-eu-silc-data), [Eurostat CROS, public use files](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/public-use-files-eurostat-microdata)
- US: the CPS-based Tax-Calculator input file is built from public survey data, while the IRS-SOI PUF is proprietary — [Tax-Calculator data docs](https://PSLmodels.github.io/Tax-Calculator/usage/data.html). PolicyEngine publishes enhanced CPS microdata on Hugging Face — [policyengine-us-data](https://policyengine.github.io/policyengine-us-data/introduction)
- Data actually used to initialise a national ABM: national and sector accounts, input-output tables, government statistics (including Eurostat government deficit/debt data), census and business demography — [Poledna et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/economic-forecasting-with-an-agent-based-model-410qdiiwpw)
- Official SAM-like data now exist for Europe: FIGARO-NAM multi-country national accounting matrices, 2025 edition — [Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-statistical-working-papers/w/ks-01-25-050)

### Inferences
- Minimum initialisation set for one country: annual and quarterly national accounts by expenditure and income; non-financial and financial sector accounts (the SFC transaction and balance-sheet matrices); government finance statistics with COFOG functional spending (the player's budget screen); supply-use / IO tables; labour-force aggregates by sector and status; income distribution by decile and household-type marginals for the synthetic population; tax-benefit parameters (statutory, from legislation or EUROMOD's CC BY rules).
- Licence strategy: (1) prefer CC BY or public-domain aggregates; (2) transform them into a calibrated game dataset and credit sources in-game; (3) never ship survey microdata or anything share-alike or non-commercial; (4) generate households synthetically from published tables. A data-provenance manifest per country is cheap insurance.
- Share-alike (EXIOBASE) is a specific hazard: derived datasets may need to be released under the same licence, which conflicts with a closed commercial data pack.
- Supporting modding ("bring your own country") is easiest if the engine ingests a documented neutral format (a SAM plus sector balance sheets plus a marginals file), since modders can then source their own data under their own licences.

## 10. Calibration and validation methods a small team can afford

### Takeaway
Run budgets scale from about 10 runs per parameter (Gaussian-process emulator design) and r(k+1) with r of 10–50 (Morris screening) up to hundreds of thousands (naive likelihood-free Bayesian inference). A deterministic millisecond-scale engine makes even the expensive end feasible on one PC, whereas a stochastic ABM core would force surrogates and cluster time.

### Cited Findings
- Emulator design rule: an effective initial computer experiment for a Gaussian-process emulator needs about n = 10d runs, where d is the number of inputs — [Loeppky, Sacks & Welch, Technometrics 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/choosing-the-sample-size-of-a-computer-experiment-a-3j4psa2v9u); the rule can be refined by tying sample size to output roughness — [Harari et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/computer-experiments-prediction-accuracy-sample-size-and-3hpqp8ar4z)
- Morris elementary-effects screening costs r(k+1) model runs for k factors, with r trajectories typically between 10 and 50 — [Campolongo, Cariboni & Saltelli 2007](https://scispace.com/papers/an-effective-screening-design-for-sensitivity-analysis-of-j7dudfgyue)
- For highly parameterised models Sobol' variance decomposition "requires a prohibitive number of model evaluations", and Morris gives sufficiently similar rankings at greatly reduced cost — [Herman et al. 2013](https://scispace.com/papers/technical-note-method-of-morris-effectively-reduces-the-2iz3twemn7); Sobol' can still be used for factor fixing and prioritisation with a limited number of runs — [Nossent et al. 2011](https://scispace.com/papers/sobol-sensitivity-analysis-of-a-complex-environmental-model-4ewj2y25oz); convergence of screening and ranking is reached before the index values themselves stabilise — [Sarrazin, Pianosi & Wagener 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/global-sensitivity-analysis-of-environmental-models-16slehg3hg)
- Likelihood-free inference: some approaches "typically require many hundreds of thousands of simulations"; neural posterior estimation and neural density-ratio estimation are more simulation-efficient black-box alternatives — [Dyer, Cannon & Farmer 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/black-box-bayesian-inference-for-agent-based-models-56jzu496i0)
- Surrogate ("meta-model") calibration: machine-learning surrogates with intelligent sampling approximate ABM behaviour and "dramatically" cut computation — [Lamperti, Roventini & Sani](https://scispace.com/papers/agent-based-model-calibration-using-machine-learning-3bj5cihjfr); random-forest surrogate search is particularly efficient and combining search methods improves results (Banca d'Italia team) — [Glielmo et al. 2023](https://scispace.com/papers/reinforcement-learning-for-combining-search-methods-in-the-4sjmjhplwn); non-parametric regression meta-models minimise distance "using relatively few simulations" — [Chen & Desiderio 2021](https://scispace.com/papers/calibration-of-agent-based-models-by-means-of-meta-modeling-3bhu0vsz8a); Gaussian-process surrogates for Bayesian estimation of large models — [Barde](https://scispace.com/papers/bayesian-estimation-of-large-scale-simulation-models-with-4kql020dop)
- For heavy ABMs the method of simulated moments is often infeasible in the loop; an alternative protocol uses nearly-orthogonal Latin hypercube sampling, stochastic kriging and an information criterion to score simulated against empirical data — [Barde & van der Hoog 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/an-empirical-validation-protocol-for-large-scale-agent-based-3uqiy7f1km)
- Bayesian estimation of a medium-scale macro ABM is possible — [Delli Gatti & Grazzini, JEBO 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/rising-to-the-challenge-bayesian-estimation-and-forecasting-3n9pm0vqyr); neural-network likelihood approximations improve accuracy over earlier methods — [Platt 2021](https://scispace.com/papers/bayesian-estimation-of-economic-simulation-models-using-2z5q9awe6a)
- History matching with probabilistic emulators iteratively rules out implausible regions of parameter space using an implausibility measure, for simulators too expensive to run densely — [Garbuno-Inigo et al. 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/history-matching-with-probabilistic-emulators-and-active-3hbkm0qa1z)
- Examples of realistic budgets: 1,000 stochastic runs per country with the World Bank's MFMod — [World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099516206222334188/pdf/IDU0847415ac0dc78040150bdd90714b239a7bb9.pdf); a dynamic IO model calibrated against 115 time series — [Alleman et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/a-dynamic-disequilibrium-input-output-model-of-the-belgian-xfum6ghvcv25)
- Base-year replication is an established calibration target: an economy can be constructed so that its simulated SAM is "very close" to the observed SAM snapshot — [Jaraíz 2022](https://scispace.com/papers/an-agent-based-modeling-approach-for-real-world-economic-3i1lmw02)
- Validation practice for ABMs distinguishes calibration/estimation from output validation against stylised facts — [Fagiolo et al., LEM WP 2017/23](https://www.lem.sssup.it/WPLem/files/2017-23.pdf)

### Inferences
- A staged pipeline for a small team: (1) base-year SAM match by construction (SFC/IO shares and coefficients read off the data, zero model runs); (2) Morris screening over all free behavioural parameters, about 1,000–5,000 runs for 100 parameters; (3) impulse-response targets (multipliers, Okun, pass-through) fitted by simulated moments over the 10–20 parameters that matter, about 10^3–10^5 runs; (4) history matching or a hindcast over 10–20 years of actual data as an out-of-sample check; (5) Sobol' indices on the short list, about 10^4–10^5 runs; (6) gameplay-balance sweeps with scripted "bot" players, 10^4–10^6 runs.
- At roughly 1–10 ms per quarter, a 20-year run (80 steps) costs 0.1–1 s single-threaded, so 10^5 runs take a few hours on an 8-core desktop. Emulators and neural posterior estimation are therefore optional refinements, not prerequisites, provided the core stays deterministic and aggregate-plus-microsimulation.
- Determinism is the key enabling property: it removes the need for Monte Carlo replication within each parameter draw, which is what makes ABM calibration 10–100 times more expensive.
- The SFC redundant-equation check and SAM row/column balance are free validation tests that should run on every headless step.

### Gaps
- The standard Sobol'/Saltelli cost formula was not retrieved in-session (my recollection: N(k+2) runs for first- and total-order indices, with N commonly 500–10,000; verify).
- Exact simulation budgets used by the INET Oxford 38-country calibration and by the Banca d'Italia `black-it` toolkit were not retrievable.
- No source found on calibration practice specifically for commercial game economies.

## 11. Prior art: serious economics in games and game-like tools

### Takeaway
There is little published evidence of commercial games using real-unit, accounting-consistent national economies; the closest proven interactive precedents are system-dynamics policy simulators (En-ROADS, T21) and open tax-benefit calculators (PolicyEngine), while academic work on game economies concentrates on MMO virtual economies. Democracy 3 is studied mainly as a teaching tool, with its simplified policy process noted as a limitation.

### Cited Findings
- Democracy 3 in research and teaching: one study finds players engage with the effects of policy on a Western country's economic, social and cultural situation, while the complexity of democratic negotiation and decision processes "cannot be experienced" — [2020 study (German)](https://scispace.com/papers/unvollstandigkeit-als-didaktische-chance-sogn9p9pmb44); it has been read as "applied political philosophy" for tinkering with utopias and dystopias — [Czauderna](https://scispace.com/papers/tinkering-with-political-utopias-and-dystopias-in-democracy-c2t6mp0yfb0w); it is proposed for public-policy teaching — [Gedikkaya & Gedikkaya 2019](https://scispace.com/papers/bilgisayar-simulasyonlarinin-kamu-politikalari-ve-kamu-10hs0ky3y8), [Kalinichenko et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/use-of-political-simulators-in-developing-analytical-skills-mh5c083fop59)
- Developer commentary on Democracy's voter modelling stresses how hard it is and the role of network effects — [Cliff Harris blog, Oct 2020](https://positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/10/21/modelling-voter-happiness-in-a-video-game-is-interesting-but-also-horrendous)
- Virtual-economy research: aggregate behaviour in a large commercial virtual world follows real-world macro patterns when measured with national-accounts-style metrics — [Castronova et al. 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/as-real-as-real-macroeconomic-behavior-in-a-large-scale-4khtxttr2i); economics concepts applied to game worlds — [Lehdonvirta 2005](https://scispace.com/papers/virtual-economics-applying-economics-to-the-study-of-game-56pihwzi7v); case analyses of Diablo III's auction house and EVE Online's player-driven market identify inflation from unbalanced currency creation as a recurring pathology — [Xi 2025](https://scispace.com/papers/why-unanchored-game-economies-are-structurally-bound-to-fail-3ejdwnch2vu4)
- An agent-driven price model for role-playing games was shown to be stable and resilient enough for use — [Doran & Parberry 2012](https://scispace.com/papers/emergent-economies-for-role-playing-games-2nzidxinwt); deep reinforcement-learning agents have been used to test game economies for inflation before launch — [2022 book chapter](https://scispace.com/papers/advances-in-measuring-inflation-within-virtual-economies-1flgeit4)
- Interactive policy simulators outside games: En-ROADS gives real-time feedback on about thirty policy levers in a browser — [EGU21 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-7608.html); T21/iSDG is used by governments for scenario planning — [Pedercini et al. 2019](https://scispace.com/papers/integrated-simulation-for-national-development-planning-q8v0rlpikb)
- Open tax-benefit calculators now serve as public "what if" tools and have been adopted inside a finance ministry (HM Treasury's PolicyEngine UK record) — [gov.uk](https://gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/hmt-modelling-policy-engine)

### Inferences
- The gap in the market is real: I found no evidence of a shipped game that combines national-accounts consistency, IO production and household microsimulation. The components are each proven in interactive non-game tools, which lowers technical risk and concentrates it in integration, tuning and presentation.
- The Xi (2025) pathology, money created without a matching sink, is the game-economy version of a stock-flow inconsistency; an SFC core addresses it by construction.
- The Democracy 3 critique (policy effects are modelled, the political process is not) and Harris's remarks on voter modelling suggest the economic engine should emit household-level winners and losers that a separate political layer can consume, which is a further argument for the microsimulation layer.
- Using scripted or learning "bot players" to hunt for dominant strategies before release is established practice in game-economy testing and maps directly onto the headless-run requirement.

These should be covered from developer diaries and the organisations' own sites before the report relies on them.
