# The voters' reactions against past elections (stage S9.4)

Generated by `python tools/uk/history.py` from `history.csv`; do not edit. Sources: UK
Parliament's election results (Open Parliament Licence v3.0), ONS's regional real household
disposable income per head and the Annual Population Survey (Open Government Licence v3.0).
The method is in the tool's docstring.

## Fit

44 observations: four terms (2010-15, 2015-17, 2017-19, 2019-24) by eleven regions of
Great Britain. The change in the log-odds of the senior governing party's share of the
electorate is regressed on the change in real income growth in the year before each
election and in regional unemployment, with an effect for each term. R squared
0.959, nearly all of it the term effects (2015: -0.07, 2017: +0.43, 2019: -0.03, 2024: -0.92).

| Effect, on the governing blocs' utility | Fitted | Standard error | Judgement before | Combined (low / central / high) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Income: per unit of real income growth | 4.01 | 2.74 | 2.5 | 1.621 / 2.759 / 3.896 |
| Unemployment: per unit of the regional rate | 0.95 | 3.40 | -6.0 | -0.74 / -2.959 / -5.208 |

Regions whose real incomes grew faster swung towards the government, by an amount in the range the economic-voting literature finds, but the error is 68% of the estimate: weak evidence, which moves the judgement a little. Regional unemployment, which moved much in step across regions, cannot be told apart from zero here (t = 0.3), so the combined effect is 49% of the earlier judgement. Time in office, from the term effects alone:
-0.066 a year (standard error 0.068), against a judgement of -0.025: four
terms are too few, and 2017's and 2024's swings too large, to say more, so it and
inflation's effect are left as they were.

## Check on 2024

Fitted on the first three terms only (income 3.29, unemployment
-1.66), the regional pattern of 2019 to 2024 (each region's swing
against Great Britain's) is predicted with a correlation of -0.14. So the effects fitted on 2010 to 2019 do not predict 2024's regional pattern: something other than these measures of the economy shaped it. The fit is weak evidence, and it moves the judgements only by its weight.
