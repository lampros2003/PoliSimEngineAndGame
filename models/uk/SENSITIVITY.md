## Reading it

- GDP: world_growth (58.8bn), productivity_growth (39.0bn), labour_force_growth (28.3bn).
- Unemployment: natural_unemployment (0.50 pts), world_growth (0.25 pts), productivity_growth (0.18 pts).
- Budget balance: world_growth (28.3bn), productivity_growth (14.3bn), labour_force_growth (11.9bn).
- Debt / GDP: world_growth (3.7 pts), gilt_yield (0.4 pts), productivity_growth (0.3 pts).
- Poll rating: vote_tenure_effect (2.46 pts), vote_income_effect (0.19 pts), productivity_growth (0.13 pts).
- Seats in 2029: vote_tenure_effect (31), natural_unemployment (7), vote_unemployment_effect (4).

The factors with the largest effects are where calibration should go first; one whose sigma is large against its mu* acts through the others. The central preset is the calibration; the ranges are judgements unless the assumptions file names a source.

One caveat: the voters' regional intercepts are fitted preset by preset, with time in office already counting in the base month. Screening moves the time-in-office effect alone, so its rows include a small shift of the base month's polls (about 0.15 points for a step of one level); the presets themselves reproduce the base month exactly.
