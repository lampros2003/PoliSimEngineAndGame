# Rust wins, but the model decides

Build PoliSim's engine in Rust, but not for the reason first given: the Democracy-style relation graph is the cheapest thing in this game to compute, and the project's real risks are model design, data and scope, not speed. Democracy 3's whole simulation is roughly 220 nodes joined by 1,149 mostly linear equations, and by the research's own timing a step at that scale costs about 10 microseconds in naive C or Rust and under a millisecond in plain Python, so no engine ever had to exist before that model did. Rust still beats C, C++ and Zig here because the owner asked for bit-identical results on every machine and for parallel-safe code written largely by an AI assistant, and Rust has the strictest floating-point defaults and compile-time data-race checking of the four. The recommended architecture is a headless Rust core that compiles data files and formulas at load time into a flat plan over struct-of-arrays tables, steps monthly in a fixed phase order, keeps money and head-counts in integers, and hashes its state every step on Windows and Linux. The recommended model is four layers sharing one set of books: a six-sector stock-flow consistent core, an input-output block of 45 to 64 industries, a cohort table of 202 cells per region aligned with about 100,000 weighted synthetic households, and party blocs in a legislature fed by expected-value voting. The United Kingdom is the first country to build, provisionally, because it is the only one for which the research found a central-bank stock-flow model, an official macro model with published code, a validated dynamic input-output model and a documented voter-modelling recipe; its data licences were not verified and must be confirmed first. The owner's ballot switch from engine-first to a thin slice of everything is the best-supported decision on the list, while the combined scope is the largest risk: no published model or shipped game has assembled this combination, so each layer must enter thin and each stage must close on a numeric test.

## A 1,149-link graph never needed a fast engine

The owner's founding opinion has three parts: write a custom engine, write it in Rust or even C, and write it first so that the game's graph of relations can be simulated massively and quickly. The research supports the first two parts, rejects the third, and shows that the graph is the wrong thing to be fast at.

### Democracy's simulation is microseconds of work

Democracy 3 is small. A 2026 reverse-engineering of its shipped data files counts about 40 simulation values, 123 policies, 21 voter groups and 36 situations joined by **1,149 equations, 89% of them straight lines in a single variable**, with every value normalised to the range 0 to 1 and clamped ([democracy3-solver](https://github.com/chrisaacson69/democracy3-solver/blob/HEAD/README.md), [findings](https://github.com/chrisaacson69/democracy3-solver/blob/HEAD/notes/findings.md), [equation analysis](https://github.com/chrisaacson69/democracy3-solver/blob/HEAD/notes/layer2.md)). Democracy 4 puts an electorate of 2,000 simulated voters on top ([Cliffski's blog](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2022/03/02/on-the-visualization-of-voter-approval-distribution-in-democracy-4/)). Positech shipped that game re-parsing constants out of equation strings every turn; when Cliff Harris profiled it in 2021, caching them cut a pre-calculation pass from 1.50 s to 0.85 s, and he later described further tuning as optimising "for fun" ([Cliffski's blog, 2021](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/12/29/speeding-up-democracy-4-simulation-processing-atof-is-slow/), [Cliffski's blog, 2022](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2022/11/05/optimization-for-fun/)). The slowdowns players could see came from draw calls, not simulation ([Cliffski's blog, 2023](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2023/03/15/speeding-up-my-game-from-59fps-to-228-fps/)).

The research team's own benchmark puts numbers on this. A synthetic influence graph of 500 nodes and 5,000 links cost **8.6 µs per step in C, 12.1 µs in untuned Rust, 22.5 µs in NumPy and 727 µs in pure Python**, with all four producing bit-identical state; at one million nodes and ten million links the step took about 100 ms in C and 113 ms in Rust. These were single runs on a shared cloud machine and are orders of magnitude, not precise figures. Published numbers agree: compiled expression evaluators in Rust cost 0.16 to 0.56 µs per formula ([exmex-benchmarks](https://github.com/bertiqwerty/exmex-benchmarks)), so a graph of 30,000 arbitrary formulas, more than a hundred times Democracy's size, still steps in 5 to 17 ms on one core. Nothing about the relation graph requires an engine to exist before the model does, and nothing about it separates Rust from C.

Democracy's actual limits are structural, and they explain the owner's other ballot choices better than any performance argument. Without its clamps the network fails to settle in 2,000 iterations, with them it converges in 53, and values pinned at a clamp make further policy changes look inert; treasury and debt live in hard-coded engine code outside the moddable graph, and policy costs are abstract numbers scaled by a per-country constant ([democracy3-solver findings](https://github.com/chrisaacson69/democracy3-solver/blob/HEAD/notes/findings.md), [scope notes](https://github.com/chrisaacson69/democracy3-solver/blob/HEAD/notes/scope.md)). A 2026 mod that wanted a bond market had to ship an external program that rewrites the autosave between turns ([D4 Economics Patcher](https://github.com/vartemiou-svg/democracy4-economics-overhaul/blob/HEAD/D4_Economics_Patcher.py)). Six of roughly 1,000 shipped equations are malformed and the engine silently tolerates them ([democracy3-solver grammar](https://github.com/chrisaacson69/democracy3-solver/blob/HEAD/notes/grammar.md)). Reviewers added that political opposition barely exists outside elections ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/indie/democracy-3-pc-review)). Real units require stocks and accounting identities, which a normalised graph cannot express; that is a modelling problem, and it is where this project's ambition belongs.

### The workload that needs compiled code is the one the ballot added

Compiled, data-oriented code matters for the household sample and the calibration harness, not for the graph. The research's judgement puts the threshold for needing a compiled engine at roughly 10^5 to 10^6 individually simulated entities per step, or at thousands of calibration runs that must finish in minutes. The owner's hybrid population and the decision to calibrate against history put the project at that threshold. The one published figure for a full statutory tax-benefit system in Rust, about **0.1 ms per household** ([policyengine-uk-rust](https://policyengine.github.io/policyengine-uk-rust)), would make a monthly step over 100,000 households take ten seconds, while the research timed a toy two-instrument system at 1.2 ms for the same sample in NumPy. That spread of four orders of magnitude, driven by rule depth and evaluation style, is the project's real performance unknown. The owner's instinct that speed must be designed in is sound; its target should move from the graph to the per-household rule kernel and to headless run throughput.

### Engine-first loses on the record; a few engine properties must still come first

The game-development record on ordering is one-sided. Introversion shelved Subversion after about six years because too much time had gone into technology and there was "a massive gaping hole" where the game should have been ([MCV/Develop](https://www.mcvuk.com/subversion-dropped-after-six-years-development/)). In a survey of 20 postmortems, over-ambitious scope and feature creep each appeared in **75% of projects, against 60% for technology problems** ([Petrillo et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/what-went-wrong-a-survey-of-problems-in-game-development-3m7iflo7ak)). The two open-source Victoria 2 remakes form a natural experiment. OpenVic built a separate deterministic simulation library with its own entity-component system, and nearly four years on a third-party index still lists the game as unplayable, a soft data point because the listing could be stale ([OpenVic-Simulation](https://github.com/OpenVicProject/OpenVic-Simulation), [osgameclones](https://osgameclones.com/victoria-ii)). Project Alice froze its design, refused improvements until feature-complete, and reached a public alpha in ten months ([Project Alice](https://github.com/schombert/Project-Alice/blob/main/docs/about.md), [November 2023 devlog](https://github.com/schombert/Project-Alice/blob/main/docs/Devlogs/2023-11/log_en.md)). Factorio chose its language on day 12 and did its optimisation years later against real factories ([Friday Facts #184](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-184)). Victoria 3's late-game slowdown came from pops fragmenting through migration, a property of the model that no pre-built engine would have predicted, and Paradox fixed it by changing the model ([Dev Diary #105](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-dev-diary-105-migration-in-1-6.1624226/)).

What the owner had right is that a few properties cannot be bolted on. A former Paradox tech lead writes that Victoria 3's planned task-graph multithreading "didn't work. At all" once more than one worker was enabled, because a shared mutable world made data races impossible to rule out ([Ropert, part 7](https://mropert.github.io/2025/10/06/making_games_tick_part7/)). Factorio traced a 2024 desync to two machines with different core counts ([Friday Facts #415](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-415)), and Project Alice reports that most of its out-of-sync bugs come from multithreading, not floating point ([Project Alice devlog](https://github.com/schombert/Project-Alice/blob/main/docs/Devlogs/2024-02/log_en.md)). Determinism, data layout, the threading contract and the save format belong in the first commit. The owner's ballot answer, a thin slice of everything with the core in its final language and benchmarks deciding what to optimise, satisfies both bodies of evidence: the core is the engine from day one, and breadth waits for a profile.

## Rust is the right language, for determinism and safety rather than speed

### The language question, settled

The owner leaned Rust and deferred to the research. The research confirms Rust, on grounds different from raw performance.

| Criterion | Rust 1.100 | C (C23) | C++ (C++23) | Zig 0.16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speed on this workload | Within a few percent of C; bounds checks cost 0 to 8% and vanish in iterator-style loops | Baseline | Baseline | Not ruled out |
| Cross-machine float determinism | Specified IEEE semantics, no implicit fused multiply-add, no fast-math | Achievable; depends on correct flags per compiler and per translation unit | Same as C | Not verified |
| Parallel safety | Data races rejected at compile time | Manual | Manual | Manual |
| Bindings needed here | godot-rust, PyO3 and maturin, cbindgen | Plain headers | godot-cpp | Not researched |
| Stability over a long project | Stable; pin the toolchain | Very stable | Stable; module tooling unfinished | Pre-1.0, standard library still breaking |
| Verdict | **Choose** | Viable fallback | Viable, no advantage | Reject |

Measured evidence puts idiomatic Rust within a few percent of C and C++ for this kind of code: checked indexing cost 2.5% to 7.7% in a research study and noise to a few percent in a production codebase ([NADER paper](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~npopescu/papers/nader.pdf), [ReadySet](https://readyset.io/blog/bounds-checks)). The 40% gap between C and Rust in the research team's small benchmark came from untuned, bounds-checked indexing and is not a language verdict. C therefore buys nothing in speed. C is a legitimate choice, since Box2D v3 ships multithreading, SIMD and cross-platform determinism together in portable C17 ([Box2D](https://github.com/erincatto/box2d)), but it offers no safety net for AI-written code and makes determinism depend on build flags being right everywhere.

Determinism is where Rust separates itself. Rust specifies that basic float operations match IEEE 754 exactly and that the compiler never contracts `a*b + c` into a fused multiply-add, with fast-math available only through separate opt-in types ([RFC 3514](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/text/3514-float-semantics.md)). Its remaining hole, transcendental functions that defer to the platform maths library, closes by using a pure-Rust implementation instead ([Rust users forum](https://users.rust-lang.org/t/on-getting-floating-points-deterministic/142232)). Parallel safety is the second separator: the Victoria 3 failure was a failure to prove that two tasks did not write the same data, which is the property Rust's ownership rules check mechanically.

Zig loses on project risk. It is still pre-1.0, its April 2026 release rebuilt the standard library's I/O interface, and its largest user, Bun, was ported to Rust in 2026 ([JetBrains interview](https://blog.jetbrains.com/blog/2026/06/05/why-zig-isn-t-1-0-yet/), [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/bun-ai-rewrite-zig-rust-4-months/)). C++ is viable, and both Victoria 2 remakes use it, but it brings no advantage for a solo developer who is equally comfortable in Rust, and its module tooling remains unfinished ([modules in 2026](https://wrocpp.github.io/posts/modules-2026/)).

Rust's costs are real and should be planned for. Compile times are the main one, and the parallel compiler front end still needs nightly ([Cargo guide](https://doc.rust-lang.org/cargo/guide/build-performance.html)). Portable SIMD is nightly-only ([portable-simd](https://github.com/rust-lang/portable-simd)). Two stable releases in 2026 needed point fixes for miscompilations, one of them affecting `bool` returns across the C boundary, so the toolchain must be pinned and upgraded deliberately ([Rust releases](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/releases)).

The evidence on AI assistance by language is thin and mixed, and it does not overturn the choice. One benchmark shows a 2025 model resolving 45.8% of Python issues but 6.7% of Rust issues, while another ranks Rust highest among its languages ([Multi-SWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.02605), [SWE-bench Multilingual](https://swebench.com/multilingual)). A Rust-specific benchmark names the two failure causes as repository-wide comprehension and Rust's strict trait semantics ([Rust-SWE-bench](https://scispace.com/papers/evaluating-and-improving-automated-repository-level-rust-01x3iy175hns)), which argues for small modules and plain types over clever generic abstractions. Against that, sixteen parallel Claude agents produced a roughly 100,000-line C compiler in Rust in two weeks, on the stated condition that "the task verifier is nearly perfect" ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)). Rust's compiler works as an extra verifier that turns an assistant's mistakes into compile errors; that is reasoning, not a measured result, and no controlled comparison of Rust, C, C++ and Zig for AI-written numerical code was found.

### Flat tables, a compiled plan and a fixed phase order

State lives in plain struct-of-arrays tables, one per record type (persons, households, cohort cells, industries, sectors, regions, parties, districts), linked by 32-bit indices instead of pointers. An entity-component framework is unnecessary: when every record of a type has the same columns, archetype storage degenerates to a single table and adds only overhead, and published ECS benchmarks already spend 3 to 8 ns per entity per system on trivial work ([ecs_benchmark](https://github.com/abeimler/ecs_benchmark)). Index-based links also remove most borrow-checker friction and make state trivially serialisable. Fields touched every step sit in dense, narrow columns of small integer codes and fixed-point values, with identifiers, history and text kept apart, because large passes are bound by memory bandwidth, not arithmetic. The number of records is fixed for the life of a game: people enter and leave by transferring weight between records, never by splitting, which designs out the Victoria 3 failure. Aggregate equations sit in dense arrays with edges in compressed-row form.

The engine is a small compiler. At load it parses data files and formulas into a typed expression graph, checks units, folds constants, finds strongly connected components and requires every cycle to pass through a stock or an explicit lag, then sorts what remains into levels and emits flat register bytecode. Two evaluators run that bytecode: a scalar one for the few hundred to few thousand aggregate equations, and a batched one that runs each opcode as a tight loop over a block of a few thousand column values for per-household rules. Batching is what keeps user-defined rules fast without a just-in-time compiler: a scalar interpreter at 0.2 µs per expression is ample for aggregates and too slow across a hundred thousand households with dozens of rules, where it would eat most of a second per step. A short list of native operators handles the algorithms that formulas express badly (cohort ageing, weight alignment, input-output propagation, seat allocation), and model files instantiate and wire them.

Execution is discrete-time and synchronous. One step is one month, the player advances one or three steps, and slow processes run on yearly phases. Each step runs phases in a fixed order: apply player commands, demography, household transitions and alignment, taxes and benefits, production and prices, sector accounts, behavioural adjustment, politics, events, then a state hash. Every phase reads only last step's values or earlier phases' outputs and declares the tables it reads and writes. That declaration is the cure a Paradox veteran proposes for the Victoria 3 problem ([Ropert, part 7](https://mropert.github.io/2025/10/06/making_games_tick_part7/)), and it makes later parallelism a scheduling change instead of a rewrite. The simulation owns its own thread, takes serialised commands in and hands immutable snapshots out, the pattern Project Alice uses ([game loop notes](https://github.com/schombert/Project-Alice/blob/main/docs/game_loop_basics.md)).

Start single-threaded with no explicit SIMD. Add parallel loops only when a benchmark demands them, over fixed index ranges with partial results combined in range order. GPU compute is out: the leading agent framework is AGPL and CUDA-first ([FLAME GPU 2](https://github.com/FLAMEGPU/FLAMEGPU2)), and shader float rules permit reassociation, which breaks bit-identity across vendors ([WGSL specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/wgsl/)). A dataframe engine as the live store is also out, because it would hand control of aggregation order to a fast-moving dependency.

### Bit-identical by construction, verified by hash

Strict cross-machine reproducibility is achievable, since Box2D v3, Rapier and Factorio ship it and OpenVic is built around it, but it is a standing constraint, not a feature added once. IEEE basic operations are reproducible across compliant hardware; divergence comes from everything around them. The approach has six rules.

Count things in integers and rate things in floats. Money in minor units, head-counts and sample weights in fixed-point integers, accumulated in 128-bit sums, make every total exact and independent of order and thread count; splitting an integer flow by a share leaves remainders, which a fixed allocation rule assigns so that identities stay exact. OpenVic goes further and bans floats from simulation code entirely ([OpenVic determinism](https://github.com/OpenVicProject/OpenVic-Simulation/blob/master/docs/ecs/determinism.md)), but Factorio's developers found fixed point impractical for most things ([Factorio forum](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=113656)), so rates, elasticities and opinions stay in `f64`. Never call the platform maths library: Factorio's few cross-platform float bugs came from functions such as `sin`, and Project Alice reimplemented its trigonometry for the same reason ([Project Alice multiplayer notes](https://github.com/schombert/Project-Alice/blob/main/docs/multiplayer_technical.md)). The formula language exposes only functions the engine implements itself, preferably correctly rounded ones, whose results are defined mathematically instead of by one code base ([CORE-MATH](https://reliable-computing.org/correctrounding.html)). Fix every reduction order by data, never by scheduler. Use a counter-based random generator keyed by seed, stream, entity and step, a family that passes the standard statistical batteries and needs no shared state ([Random123](https://github.com/DEShawResearch/random123)), and prefer expected values to random draws wherever the model allows. Never iterate an unordered map in simulation code. Hash the full state every step and compare the hashes in continuous integration on Windows and Linux from the first week, adding Factorio's "heavy mode" of saving, reloading and re-hashing every step ([Friday Facts #315](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-315)).

The guarantee should be scoped honestly: the same engine build and the same model hash on 64-bit x86 Windows and Linux, which is the owner's whole platform list. The price is visible in other projects: Rapier's cross-platform mode cannot be combined with its SIMD features ([Rapier determinism](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/determinism)). The payoff is larger than replays. Determinism removes Monte Carlo replication from calibration, makes golden-run tests exact, turns a command log into a save, a bug report and a future lockstep multiplayer protocol, and gives AI-written code the near-perfect verifier it needs.

Saves are a versioned columnar snapshot plus the command log since that snapshot, with the engine build, model hash and ordered mod list in the header. Write columns as raw little-endian arrays and small structured parts with a serialiser that has a documented stable wire format ([postcard](https://github.com/jamesmunns/postcard)); avoid bincode, declared unmaintained in December 2025 ([RUSTSEC-2025-0141](https://api.osv.dev/v1/vulns/RUSTSEC-2025-0141)).

## Four model layers share one set of books

### Why this hybrid, and what was rejected

The owner's two hybrids match what the evidence favours. Stock-flow consistent models are compact systems of difference equations built on balance-sheet and transaction matrices, and their accounting "reduces the degrees of freedom of the model" ([Nikiforos and Zezza](https://levyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/wp_891.pdf)); the Bank of England built one for the UK with seven sectors on official data ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2016/a-dynamic-model-of-financial-balances-for-the-uk)). A dynamic, non-equilibrium input-output model forecast UK sectoral output accurately in real time during 2020 ([Pichler et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9472492)). Tax-benefit microsimulation applies coded rules to a weighted household sample and answers the question a political game most needs answered: who wins and who loses.

The alternatives lose for specific reasons. Full computable general equilibrium presumes market clearing every period, and its canonical database now costs for-profit users in high-income economies US$10,000 ([GTAP pricing](https://gtap.agecon.purdue.edu/databases/pricing.asp)). One-to-one agent-based economies need clusters, with 331 million agents taking 108 seconds on 128 cores ([Gill et al.](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/17104)), and their calibration can need hundreds of thousands of simulations ([Dyer et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/black-box-bayesian-inference-for-agent-based-models-56jzu496i0)); one replication found that scale "is not relevant for the forecasting performances" anyway ([Di Domenico et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/a-large-scale-data-driven-agent-based-model-for-the-italian-2jpoaqwrks)). Institutional semi-structural models are the right source of borrowed equations and validation targets, not a core.

### Sizes and structure

| Layer | Representation | Recommended size | Step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demographic backbone | Cohort table: single-year age 0 to 100 by sex by region | 202 cells per region (about 2,400 at 12 regions, 10,100 at 50) | Monthly flows, yearly ageing |
| Household sample | Weighted persons nested in households, fixed count | 20,000 households in the first slice; **about 100,000 households (roughly 220,000 persons)** as default; benchmarked to 10^6 persons | Monthly |
| Sector accounts | Six sectors: households, non-financial firms, banks, central bank, government, rest of world | Dozens of equations at first, low hundreds at maturity | Monthly, recursive |
| Production | Input-output with partial adjustment | 10 to 15 aggregated industries first; then the open table's resolution, 45 to 50 (OECD) or 64 (FIGARO) | Monthly |
| External | Rest of world, later blocs | One at first; four or five blocs later | Monthly |
| Policy and outcome graph | Typed nodes with units for non-economic domains | A few hundred nodes, Democracy scale | Monthly |
| Politics | Parties, districts, legislature seats, about 16 issue axes | Up to a dozen parties; districts as the country defines them | Monthly polls, scheduled elections |

The economic sizes are set by data and legibility, not by compute. The open tables cap industry detail: the OECD's inter-country tables carry 45 to 50 industries for 80 economies and Eurostat's FIGARO has been used at 64 sectors ([OECD](https://t4.oecd.org/sti/ind/inter-country-input-output-tables.htm), [Jaraíz and Pinacho](https://scispace.com/papers/deployers-an-agent-based-modeling-tool-for-multi-country-3d62jhfl4q1i)). The research team timed a direct solve at 64 sectors at about 70 µs, so resolution is an authoring question, and the interface should show 10 to 20 sectors whatever the engine runs. Start with one rest-of-world sector, as the Bank of England's model has, and split it into four or five blocs once trade behaves; a bloc is a row of demand growth, prices and interest rates plus trade shares, not a model of its own. The research has little direct evidence on bloc count, so that number is a judgement. Regions follow the country's real statistical regions, as the owner specified; they carry people, labour markets, household records and votes from the start, while production stays national and is apportioned by regional employment shares until regional accounts have been examined. A Democracy-style graph of a few hundred typed nodes remains for the non-economic domains such as health, crime, education and the environment. It reads real quantities from the other layers, such as spending per head or unemployment, and feeds back through rates those layers own, such as mortality or productivity.

The cohort table owns head-counts. Cohort-component projection is the universal official method but is "limited to a small number of characteristics" ([DYNAMIS-POP documentation](https://documentation.ihsn.org/dynamis/DYNAMIS-POP-MRT-2017_v01_01_Chapter1.html)), so it stays at age, sex and region; crossing ten attributes in a cell table would produce millions of mostly empty cells, where a sample grows by one column per attribute. The household sample owns the joint distribution of everything else: education, industry, labour status, income components, opinions, party attachment. Official dynamic microsimulations run on samples, not full populations: 100,000 persons in DYNASIM3, about 553,000 in Belgium's MIDAS, 7 to 9 million census records in Canada's Demosim ([Li and O'Donoghue survey](https://microsimulation.pub/articles/00082), [MIDAS](https://assets.vlaanderen.be/image/upload/v1698132727/MIDAS_qhmpnm.pdf), [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/pub/91-551-x/2010001/meth-eng.htm)). INSEE states the trade plainly: a modest sample "allows for rapid simulation and thus the creation of numerous variants, at the cost of lower accuracy" ([INSEE](https://www.insee.fr/en/information/8659672)).

The two population layers are joined by alignment. Each step the sample ages and undergoes its transitions, then record weights are rescaled so that weighted counts match the cohort table's cells. Deaths and emigration are weight decay, and births and immigration are weight added to recycled records, so the record count never changes. The literature supports each piece: a microsimulation can reproduce a cohort-component projection exactly ([DYNAMIS-POP documentation](https://documentation.ihsn.org/dynamis/DYNAMIS-POP-MRT-2017_v01_01_Chapter1.html)), a simplified microsimulation diverged from its cohort benchmark by as much as 20% in one comparison ([International Journal of Microsimulation](https://microsimulation.pub/articles/00289)), and a weight can be carried as an ordinary variable on the record ([Dekkers and Cumpston](https://microsimulation.pub/articles/00072)). Pure reweighting comes to rely excessively on small groups in volatile periods ([JASSS](https://www.jasss.org/17/4/12.html)), so the engine monitors effective sample size per region and periodically splits heavy records and merges light ones; that remedy is a design judgement, not a sourced method. No published game uses this design, and it is assembled from official-statistics practice.

The sample size follows from precision, not from realism. Ordinary sampling arithmetic gives a standard error of about 0.5 percentage points on a 30% share at 10,000 equally weighted records, which is enough for national decile tables and budget costings. Regional and district results need more, and 100,000 households gives them room. A one-to-one population adds no statistical information once outcomes are computed as expectations, at ten to a hundred times the memory and time. At the research's estimate of about 64 bytes per person the default sample occupies some 14 MB, and at its estimated 30 to 100 ns per person per step the demographic and opinion passes cost 7 to 22 ms on one core. Those per-step figures are engineering estimates anchored on published epidemic simulators such as Covasim, which reaches about 7 million person-days per core-second in array-based Python ([Covasim](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1009149)); they were not measured. Because records carry weights, sample size is a load-time setting: calibration sweeps and in-game forecasts run on a thinned sample of every tenth household at ten times the weight.

### How the layers post to the same ledger

Every money flow in every layer is a posting between two sector accounts, and the transaction-flow matrix is the join between layers. Top-down linkage, in which macro aggregates drive the micro sample, is the most commonly used approach in institutional practice ([Feltenstein et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/the-impact-of-micro-simulation-and-cge-modeling-on-tax-f4hrettqwx)); this design adds one lagged bottom-up return. Top-down, last step's wages, prices and employment by industry and region rescale earnings in the sample and drive its labour-status transitions, aligned so that sample employment matches the production block. The tax and benefit rules then run over every household, and a consumption rule turns disposable income into spending by product group. Bottom-up, the weighted totals of taxes, benefits and consumption are the household column of the transaction matrix; the macro layer has no separate aggregate consumption function to disagree with them. Consumption by product, government purchases, investment and exports form final demand; the input-output block converts demand into industry output, employment, imports and cost-push prices; value added splits into wages, profits and taxes that post back to the sectors; and stocks of debt, deposits and capital update from the flows.

Three design rules keep this safe. First, the bottom-up feedback is lagged one step, which removes the iterative macro-micro loop whose "convergence is not guaranteed" ([Cockburn et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/h/eme/ceazzz/s0573-855520140000293008.html)) and lets the whole step evaluate in dependency order with no within-period solver. Monthly steps make that lag a month instead of the quarter it would cost in a conventional model. Second, the accounts need a supply side. SFC models typically have a demand-led closure ([Nikiforos and Zezza](https://levyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/wp_891.pdf)), so without capacity limits, a labour constraint and a wage-price block, stimulus always works and the game has a dominant strategy. Third, the redundant accounting identity, the one implied by all the others, is asserted on every step of every headless run; with integer money it holds exactly. The linkage is itself a modelling assumption, since the same reform linked three ways gives different distributional answers ([Colombo](https://scispace.com/papers/linking-cge-and-microsimulation-models-a-comparison-of-2zwafynf2k)), and it should be documented and tested as such.

The owner's decision to ship disputed parameters as low, central and high settings is well founded. Spending multipliers cluster between 0.6 and 1 on average and run higher in downturns ([Ramey](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25531), [Gechert and Rannenberg](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/fiscal-multipliers-downturns-and-effects-eurozone-consolidation)), and estimates of the elasticity of taxable income span 0.074 to 0.827 ([Neisser](https://epflicht.ulb.uni-bonn.de/download/pdf/698662)). Many multipliers will emerge from structure, through import leakage, saving and the central-bank rule, so the calibration target is that the central preset reproduces published ranges in headless impulse tests.

### Parties and a legislature as blocs, voters as expected values

Voters are the household sample. Each person carries positions on about sixteen issue axes, party attachment and turnout propensity, and each party a position vector and a valence term driven by the economy, competence and time in office. Vote choice is a multinomial logit over parties plus abstention, and each person contributes a probability vector times a weight to a district-by-party tally instead of a sampled vote. Expected-value tallies make elections deterministic and remove sampling noise, which matters because, by ordinary sampling arithmetic, a sampled constituency of 1,000 records carries about three points of noise. The structure follows the best-validated empirical practice: demographic regression and post-stratification called 93% of seats in the 2017 UK election ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/18266-how-yougov-model-2017-general-election-works)), and the forecasting literature centres on the economy, incumbent popularity and time in office ([PS: Political Science & Politics](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/3B0CD7678126F613C38AA011D3C034E2)). Social-influence opinion dynamics lack validation against real data ([JASSS](https://www.jasss.org/27/1/17.html)) and belong only as a slow drift term. For countries with hundreds of single-member districts, a district-by-voter-type count matrix in the post-stratification style gives seat results without requiring a thousand sampled records per seat; that is a design inference from the YouGov method.

In the engine the political layer is ordinary tables and formulas plus two native operators. A party is a row holding seats, positions, discipline and its relation to the government. A bill is a queued policy command that must win a vote: each bloc's support is a formula of the distance between bill and party position, coalition status and polling incentive, and the bill passes if seat-weighted support clears the threshold. Seat allocation and coalition scoring are native operators parameterised by data, so electoral systems are content. Individual legislators, ministers, factions and media stay out, as the ballot decided. One caution applies to this whole subsection: the research retrieved no sources on electoral formulas or coalition theory before its search budget ran out, so the mechanics rest on unverified textbook knowledge and need sourcing before implementation.

### Build the United Kingdom first, behind a licence gate

The research contains no dedicated country comparison, so this recommendation is a synthesis. The United Kingdom has the deepest stack of published, checkable reference models in the notes: the Bank of England's seven-sector stock-flow model on official data ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/working-paper/2016/a-dynamic-model-of-financial-balances-for-the-uk)); the Office for Budget Responsibility's macro model with published code, updated in October 2025, and its ready reckoners as validation targets ([OBR model](https://obr.uk/forecasts-in-depth/obr-macroeconomic-model/), [OBR ready reckoner](https://articles.obr.uk/the-obr-ready-reckoner)); the validated dynamic input-output model cited above; an open tax-benefit rules engine that the Treasury itself uses, valuable as a reference though its AGPL licence bars linking it into a closed game ([HM Treasury record](https://gov.uk/algorithmic-transparency-records/hmt-modelling-policy-engine), [PolicyEngine UK](https://www.github.com/PolicyEngine/policyengine-uk)); an open synthetic population of England as a cross-check ([Synthetic Population Catalyst](https://alan-turing-institute.github.io/uatk-spc/)); and the published voter-modelling recipe with named public inputs. OECD content published since July 2024 is CC BY 4.0 by default ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2024/07/oecd-data-publications-and-analysis-become-freely-accessible.html)), which should cover the current inter-country input-output tables, though no dataset-specific statement was found. It is a parliamentary system in which the player is naturally head of government, and its sources are in English, which matters when an AI assistant and one reviewer must check every equation.

The choice has three weaknesses. The UK statistical office's reuse terms were not researched and the household survey behind UK microsimulation is restricted, so the population must be synthetic and the licence check gates the choice. Its 650 single-member seats force district-level electoral geography beyond the 10 to 50 economic regions. And first-past-the-post rarely produces coalitions, so the coalition mechanics the owner asked for would be under-exercised. The United States has the fully published FRB/US model and a public survey-based microdata file ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-python.htm), [Tax-Calculator](https://PSLmodels.github.io/Tax-Calculator/usage/data.html)), but its presidential, two-party, federal structure fits neither "head of government with a legislature and coalitions" nor a single regional tier. The second country should therefore be an EU state with proportional representation, built on the harmonised Eurostat pipeline of FIGARO tables, EUROPOP projections, CC BY tax-benefit rules from EUROMOD and synthetic public-use survey files ([EUROMOD](https://euromod-web.jrc.ec.europa.eu/download-euromod), [Eurostat public-use files](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/puf-eu-silc-data)). Austria is the strongest candidate, because the leading data-driven macro model was built for it from national and sector accounts, input-output tables, government statistics and census data ([Poledna et al.](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/18339)), and it is among the first countries with a synthetic public-use survey file. If the owner knows another country's institutions far better, that knowledge outweighs these arguments, since knowing what the model should compute is the scarce input. The test any candidate must pass is the same: redistributable aggregate tables, published reference models to validate against, a parliamentary system, and a real regional tier inside the owner's band.

Data handling follows one rule regardless of country: ship derived parameters and a synthetic population generated from published marginals, never raw third-party tables or survey microdata, and keep a provenance manifest per input. Avoid share-alike sources such as EXIOBASE, whose CC BY-SA terms can oblige derived datasets to carry the same licence ([EXIOBASE](https://exiobase.iedl.no)).

## Model files with units, a Godot shell and a Python lab

### The authoring and modding format

The owner's choice of data files plus a small formula language compiled at load time is the option the evidence favours. It is the only approach in which the model stays a data structure, so unit checking, dependency graphs, generated documentation and sensitivity analysis come cheaply. Democracy shows the cost of the alternative: hand-typed equation strings with no validator produced errors in the shipped game and in published mods. Paradox shows the cost of an undocumented script surface: the community had to build its own validator for it ([CWTools](https://github.com/cwtools/cwtools)).

The concrete shape is structured text for definitions and plain tables for bulk numbers. The research found no evidence favouring one file syntax; TOML with a published schema is a sound default, and the schema matters more than the syntax. Large numeric tables such as input-output coefficients, mortality rates and household seeds go in CSV or Parquet with declared columns, never formulas in table cells, which is where Democracy's format broke. A sketch, with placeholder values:

```toml
[var.gov_debt]
kind = "stock"                      # stocks change only through declared flows
unit = "GBP"
sector = "government"
inflow = "gov_deficit"

[var.income_tax_due]
entity = "person"                   # evaluated column-wise over the sample
unit = "GBP/month"
expr = "bracket_tax(taxable_income, param.income_tax_bands)"

[param.import_propensity]
unit = "1"
low = 0.25                          # disputed parameters ship as three values
central = 0.30
high = 0.35
source = "citation key"

[[patch]]
target = "base:var.income_tax_due"  # a mod edits one node, never a whole file
op = "replace_expr"
expr = "bracket_tax(taxable_income, param.flat_tax_bands)"
```

Every variable declares a kind (stock, flow, auxiliary, parameter, policy lever), a unit, its dimensions (region, industry, sector, age), an expected range and a source. Formulas are pure and total, at spreadsheet level: arithmetic, comparisons, clamps, lookup tables, lags, moving averages (Democracy's "inertia"), sums over a dimension, and weighted aggregation from person to household to region to nation. Money flows declare a paying and a receiving sector, so the loader generates the accounting identities instead of trusting authors to write them. Unit checking must be the engine's own run-time checker, strict in developer mode, because compile-time unit crates cannot see mod content; Vensim's long-standing checker is non-blocking and matches unit names literally, a weakness to avoid ([Vensim](https://vensim.com/documentation/ref_units_check.html)).

Three loader practices come from the best-regarded modding systems. Loading is staged and the model is frozen after load, as in Factorio ([Factorio data lifecycle](https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/auxiliary/data-lifecycle.html)). Mods patch named nodes instead of overwriting files, as RimWorld learned after whole-file overrides let the last mod silently win ([RimWorld wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Modding_Tutorials/PatchOperations)). Every element has a stable namespaced identifier, and saves record the rename migrations already applied, as Factorio's do ([Factorio migrations](https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/auxiliary/migrations.html)). Hot reload recompiles the plan at a step boundary and carries state across by identifier. The base game is the first mod, which costs nothing if adopted on day one.

Do not add a scripting tier now. Because the language is total and has no I/O, mods are deterministic by construction, whereas Factorio had to strip Lua's libraries and replace its maths functions to get the same property ([Factorio Lua libraries](https://lua-api.factorio.com/1.1.0/Libraries.html)). If demand for code mods appears, sandboxed WebAssembly is the candidate, since a deterministic interpreter exists under permissive licences ([wasmi](https://github.com/wasmi-labs/wasmi)). The one lesson to respect from Democracy is that a moddable surface that stops at fixed columns blocks new mechanics, so mods must be able to declare new stocks, flows and sectors, not only new rows. An exporter to the open XMILE standard for the stock-flow subset is a cheap later addition that allows independent re-simulation in existing tools ([XMILE](https://docs.oasis-open.org/xmile/xmile/v1.0/xmile-v1.0.html)).

### Client integration

Godot 4 with the engine as a native extension is one of the two routes with the strongest current evidence, the other being a web-technology shell. Godot, at version 4.7.2 since August 2026 ([Godot releases](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/releases)), is MIT-licensed, its extension interface is forward-compatible across minor versions ([godot-cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-cpp)), and Slay the Spire 2 sold three million units in a week on it in 2026 ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/03/the-godot-powered-slay-the-spire-2-has-already-hit-over-3-million-sales)). The decision stands, with four cautions. Godot has no built-in chart control and the main community add-on describes itself as "still evolving" ([Easy Charts](https://github.com/fenix-hub/godot-engine.easy-charts)). Its node-graph control is still flagged experimental ([Godot documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_graphedit.html)). Screen-reader support is new and experimental ([Godot 4.5](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.5/)). And dense interfaces are a risk in their own right: Football Manager 26's rebuilt interface drew reviews calling its menus a "tangled mess" ([Top Gear](https://www.topgear.com/car-news/gaming/football-manager-26-review-fantastic-match-engine-tangled-mess-menus)), and Project Alice's lead concluded that "the user interface *is* the game" ([Project Alice devlog](https://github.com/schombert/Project-Alice/blob/main/docs/Devlogs/2024-01/log_en.md)). Budget for writing custom chart controls, and prototype the densest screen early. The alternatives were weighed and lose for now: Bevy shipped three breaking releases in ten months of 2026 and still has no editor ([Bevy releases](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/releases)), and a Tauri web shell has an unresolved Steam overlay problem on Windows ([Tauri issue 6196](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/6196)). A web shell over a WebAssembly build of the core becomes the better route only if a browser version ever matters, which the ballot excluded.

The binding uses godot-rust, currently v0.5.5 with support for the Godot 4.7 interface ([godot-rust releases](https://github.com/godot-rust/gdext/releases)). Its MPL-2.0 licence lets game code stay closed ([godot-rust](https://github.com/godot-rust/gdext)). Its threading support is explicitly experimental and Godot objects are not thread-safe by default ([godot-rust issue 709](https://github.com/godot-rust/gdext/issues/709)), which dictates the pattern: the extension class holds only a channel end, the simulation runs on a Rust-owned thread, and the interface thread drains finished snapshots once per frame and copies them into Godot's packed arrays. The workspace splits into a core crate with no Godot dependency and no game content, the adapter crate, a Python module, a command-line runner and the content packs. All consumers use one hand-designed surface of serialised commands in and columnar views out, with an explicit interface version; a C header over that surface is a mechanical addition for the day the engine is open-sourced. This separation keeps the client choice reversible at the cost of the interface layer only.

### Tooling

The first "throwaway interface" should be a command-line runner and a Python notebook, before any Godot screen. The Python route is mature: PyO3 0.29 with maturin builds the module ([PyO3](https://docs.rs/crate/pyo3/latest), [maturin](https://github.com/PyO3/maturin/releases)), and the Arrow interface hands run data to dataframe tools without copies ([pyo3-arrow](https://docs.rs/pyo3-arrow)). A runner that takes a scenario, a seed and a command script and writes Parquet is the single harness for golden runs, calibration sweeps, scripted bot players and bug reports.

The test suite is layered: golden runs with a state hash per step; save and reload at every step; property tests over random command sequences and random valid mod content asserting that sector accounts sum to zero, population is conserved and stocks stay in range ([proptest](https://github.com/proptest-rs/proptest)); fuzzing of the model loader; and model-level range and history checks stored beside the model, in the style SDEverywhere runs on every change to a production climate simulator ([SDEverywhere](https://github.com/climateinteractive/SDEverywhere)). Benchmarks with stated budgets run in continuous integration, because an assistant will not notice a tenfold slowdown; Divan and Tracy cover measurement and inspection ([Divan](https://github.com/nvzqz/divan), [Tracy](https://github.com/wolfpld/tracy)). The model-level tools have to be built in-house and are straightforward only because the model is data: a dependency-graph exporter, the unit checker and a generated reference page for every variable.

## Eight stages, each closed by a numeric test

### One refinement to the thin-slice decision

The ballot's build order stands. The evidence supports one addition: an executable oracle before the bulk of the Rust is generated. The agent-built compiler worked because its verifier was nearly perfect, and its authors still saw new features regularly break old ones ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)). Grounding tests in an explicit specification raised correct-code rates by 38 percentage points in one 2026 study ([Haeri and Ghelichi](https://scispace.com/papers/specification-grounding-drives-test-effectiveness-for-llm-sz3q2t056xin)). A second implementation written from a prose description diverges, in trends as well as magnitudes ([Donkin et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/replicating-complex-agent-based-models-a-formidable-task-2nqeb3qtci)). So the first stage produces a written specification and a naive reference evaluator, a few hundred lines of Python that read the same model files and compute the same step slowly. It is not a prototype to be rewritten; it is the test oracle, and the research team's benchmark showed that bit-exact agreement across Python, NumPy, C and Rust is attainable when operation order is fixed.

### The roadmap

Comparable projects took years: Songs of Syx spent about six years as a one-person prototype before Early Access, Noita about nine years from engine start to release, Terra Invicta nine years to its 1.0 ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2019/07/songs-of-syx-a-city-builder-with-empire-management-tactical-battles-and-rpg-elements), [DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/1938/1937), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Invicta)). The research found no evidence-based durations for stages like these, so the roadmap is defined by exit criteria, and only the first stage carries a time box.

| Stage | Built | Deliberately excluded | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S0 Specification, oracle, data gate** (time-boxed to about a month) | Written spec with units and 5 to 10 target patterns; model-file format v0; toy model (three-sector accounts, 1,000 synthetic households, one region); Python reference evaluator; licence check on first-country data; micro-benchmarks of a 10^6-person pass and of the tax-benefit kernel | Real data pipeline, Godot, optimisation | Identities hold exactly over 200 steps; every target pattern reproduced; licence confirmed per source in a provenance manifest; benchmark numbers recorded |
| **S1 Walking skeleton** | Rust core: loader, unit check, plan compiler, both evaluators, phased step, counter-based random numbers, state hash, snapshot and command-log saves; command-line runner; Python module; CI on Windows and Linux | Threads, SIMD, regions, real country | Engine matches the oracle bit-for-bit on 1,000 random scenarios; save, load and continue equals an uninterrupted run; identical hashes on both systems; step budget met at ten times toy size; a term is playable from the command line |
| **S2 Playable shell** | Godot client over the snapshot channel: budget screen, one chart, one cause-and-effect view; model hot reload; stylised country with real headline aggregates | Art, localisation, Workshop | Outside players finish a term unaided and each explains one causal chain; an edited formula reloads without restart |
| **S3 National books** | Real country: six-sector accounts, budget by function, cohort table, 10 to 15 industries, one rest-of-world; one-command data pipeline | Households, regions, blocs, parties | Base year reproduces published accounts by construction; a 10 to 20 year hindcast stays inside stated bands on headline aggregates |
| **S4 Households** | Synthetic population from published marginals, 20,000 then 100,000 households; tax and benefit rules as column formulas; alignment; lagged bottom-up totals | Life-course events beyond ageing and labour status; wealth dynamics | Micro totals equal the household account every step; main tax revenues within tolerance of outturn; record count fixed and effective sample size above its floor over 50 years; step within budget at 10^6 persons on a mid-range laptop |
| **S5 Politics** | Voter utilities, expected-value tallies, seat allocation, party blocs, bill votes, coalition scoring, opposition response | Ministers, factions, media, lobbies | Two or three past elections back-cast within a stated seat error; a bill can fail; scripted bots find no trivially dominant strategy |
| **S6 Depth and calibration** | Full industry resolution, regions, four or five blocs, low/central/high presets, sensitivity screening, historical-episode tests; a profile-gated optimisation slot | New mechanics | Central preset reproduces published multiplier ranges; zero divergent runs in 10,000 random-policy games; results invariant to thread count |
| **S7 Content and public surface** | Interface depth, content, generated mod documentation, migrations, Workshop, Early Access; then a second country and the engine's open-source release | — | An outside modder adds a tax and a policy from the documentation alone; previous-build saves load through recorded migrations; the second country loads with no engine change |

Playable comes before real data on purpose. Offworld Trading Company's team put a prototype in outside hands early to create a feedback loop ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/offworld-trading-company-prototype-is-now-available-as-a-founders-elite-edition-preorder-bonus)), and the Subversion failure is the absence of exactly that test. Three pivot rules should be written down now. If S0 overruns its box twice, cut the model, not the tooling. If S1 cannot match the oracle, fix determinism before adding features. If the household step misses its budget by more than ten times after one profiling pass, reduce the sample or the rule depth before optimising code.

Calibration is affordable because the core is deterministic. Sensitivity screening by the Morris method costs r(k+1) runs with r between 10 and 50, a few thousand runs for a hundred parameters ([Campolongo et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/an-effective-screening-design-for-sensitivity-analysis-of-j7dudfgyue)), and a deterministic model needs no replication within each parameter draw.

### Where the owner's decisions carry risk

| Decision | What the evidence says | Main risk | How to stage it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thin slice of everything (10) | Strongly supported | Throwaway interface hardens; AI code drifts without a verifier | Oracle in S0; exit criteria, not line counts |
| Bit-identical everywhere (13) | Achievable and shipped elsewhere; an enabler for calibration, tests and AI work | Permanent limits on SIMD, threading and maths | Scope to x86-64 Windows and Linux at one build and model hash; hash in CI from week one |
| Hybrid population (05) | Standard official practice; no game precedent | Alignment drift, weight degeneracy | Reweighting-only ageing first; fixed record count; monitor effective sample size |
| Hybrid economy (06) | Components published piecewise; the triple is not | Contested behavioural equations; the linkage is an assumption; demand-led bias | National accounts first; lagged recursive solve; supply block before any balancing |
| 10 to 50 regions (07) | Multiplies cohort cells and sample needs | Regional economic data were not researched | Regions carry people, labour and votes first; production by regional shares |
| Parties and legislature (04) | Answers a documented Democracy weakness; computationally trivial | Mechanics unsourced in the research; design effort | Party blocs only; after the economy works |
| Monthly steps (03) | Workable | Reference models and accounts are quarterly | Validate on quarterly aggregates; convert adjustment speeds explicitly |
| First-class modding (17) | Format well supported | Public interface frozen too early | Base game as a mod from day one; stability promise only at Early Access |
| Godot client (15) | Supported with caveats | Charts and dense interfaces | Simulation thread plus snapshots; prototype the densest screen in S2 |
| Solo, part-time, AI-assisted (19, 20) | Productivity gains are conditional | Over-estimated speed; regressions | Small modules; budgets in CI; plan in years |

The scope warning deserves plain words. The research found no published model that combines stock-flow accounts, input-output production and household microsimulation in one open system, and no game that pairs cohort tables with an aligned weighted sample. The pieces exist only in pairs, such as a stock-flow input-output model with an arbitrary number of sectors ([Berg et al.](https://scispace.com/papers/a-stock-flow-consistent-input-output-model-with-applications-1mva54buee)). The Bank of England's authors needed a purpose-built database for the sector accounts alone. Each layer adds its own calibration burden, and the layers multiply at their joins. A solo part-time developer attempting all of it at once walks into the scope problem that three-quarters of surveyed postmortems report. No deadline helps, but a missing deadline is also how Subversion spent six years. The mitigation is the roadmap's exclusion column: the national economy works before households exist, households before parties, one rest-of-world before blocs, twelve industries before sixty-four. Households themselves enter by reweighting alone before any life-course dynamics, which the linking literature accepts as "a good alternative" when data or time are limited ([Hérault](https://scispace.com/papers/sequential-linking-of-computable-general-equilibrium-and-3zkjdxvyhd)).

One smaller mismatch needs handling. The reference models step by quarter, including FRB/US and the Danish and Italian stock-flow models ([Brayton and Tinsley](https://scispace.com/papers/a-guide-to-frb-us-a-macroeconomic-model-of-the-united-states-1vegeu6fvl), [Byrialsen et al.](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/510735086/p_fmm_imk_wp_79_2022.pdf), [Zezza and Zezza](https://ideas.repec.org/p/lev/wrkpap/wp_958.html)), so the owner's monthly step requires converting published adjustment speeds and validating on quarterly aggregates. That is friction, not a reason to change the decision, and the shorter step makes the lagged feedback less distorting.

On AI assistance, the only randomised trial found experienced developers **19% slower** with early-2025 tools while believing they were faster ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)); a later update points toward a speed-up but its interval includes zero and the study design is confounded ([METR update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). Assistance lowers the cost of writing the engine and of a later port, which weakens the case for locking in a grand architecture now. It does not lower the cost of knowing what the model should compute.

### What the research did not verify

Web access was restricted throughout the research. Most findings were confirmed from search-result summaries, not full readings, so quotations and exact figures should be re-checked at source before they are relied on. Democracy's internals rest on a third party's reading of the shipped files, and the exact turn-update order and voting arithmetic remain unknown.

The performance estimates for the population step, 30 to 100 ns per person, are engineering estimates and were never measured; the research's own benchmark was a single synthetic kernel on a noisy virtual machine; and the 0.1 ms-per-household tax-benefit figure is search-summary level. The S0 micro-benchmarks exist to replace all three with numbers from target hardware. No head-to-head benchmark of Rust against C or C++ on simulation kernels was retrieved, and the level-scheduling design for the equation graph is standard reasoning, not a cited method.

On data and law, the country recommendation is this report's synthesis, not a researched comparison. The reuse terms of Eurostat tables rest on recollection, the UK statistical office's terms and the Family Resources Survey conditions were not researched, and the licences of the open synthetic populations were not confirmed. Whether a commercial studio may use any research-only source for offline estimation is a legal question. Marginal propensities to consume, investment elasticities and trade elasticities, all of which the model needs, were not sourced, and no published per-period solve times or confirmed equation counts were found for empirical stock-flow models.

On politics, nothing about electoral formulas, coalition theory or classic voting theory was sourced. On the client, no postmortem covers Godot with thousands of interface nodes or the cost of chart rendering, and the cost of copying snapshots into Godot arrays is unmeasured. On process, no comparable studio's milestone plan was found, stage durations have no empirical basis, and the evidence does not settle whether the reference evaluator should be retired once golden files are trusted or kept for the life of the project. The reputational risk of modelling a real country's politics was outside the research altogether.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the engine is. It is not a fast processor for a graph of relations; it is a compiler and runtime for an accounting model, and its scarce asset is the verifier: the specification, the oracle, the identities that must sum to zero and the hash that must match on two operating systems. That reframing ties the ballot together. Strict determinism, which looks like the most expensive answer on the list, is what makes AI-written code checkable, calibration affordable on a laptop, saves and replays trivial and multiplayer a later option instead of a rewrite. Rust is the right choice because it makes that one property cheapest to hold.

The ambition the owner asked to keep is best spent where no prior game exists: one set of books that a household, a ministry and a parliament all post to. The first proof to seek is therefore not a benchmark. It is a single believable causal chain, traced in real units from a tax change through a household's income to a constituency's vote, that an outside player can explain back.
